Zhacai

Zhacai (tige de moutarde confite au piment)

Latin nameBrassica juncea var. tumida
FamilyVegetables
OriginFuling, Chongqing, China
SeasonAll year
Flavoursalty · sour · pungent · hot
Typical price8–15 €/kg

What it is

Commercial production started at Fuling on the Yangtze in 1898, and the name records the method rather than the plant: zha means pressed, after the tofu press first used to squeeze the brine out of the salted stems. What is preserved is the swollen stem of a mustard, not its leaves.

In the kitchen

It is sold heavily salted under a chilli coating: rinse the chilli off, slice thin, then soak 10 to 20 minutes and taste before you use it. Treat it as the salt in a dish — a small handful shredded into a pork and noodle soup replaces the seasoning entirely.

Goes with

Pork Silken tofu (kinugoshi) Rice Toasted sesame oil Spring onion Ginger
