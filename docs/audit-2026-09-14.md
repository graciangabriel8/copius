# Factual audit — 14 September 2026

What was checked, against which source, and what changed. Every verdict below
came from a page or dataset actually opened; nothing rests on a search-engine
summary. `UNVERIFIED` appears where no source could be opened, and is left as
`UNVERIFIED` rather than guessed.

## Method

Three classes of claim in the atlas are falsifiable against a public register.
Each was checked against the register itself, downloaded whole, rather than
looked up item by item:

| class | count | source |
|---|---|---|
| protected designations | 176 | EU geographical-indications register (3 980 records) + Swiss federal AOP/IGP register (42) |
| latin names | 1 335 | GBIF taxonomic backbone |
| Michelin star claims | 45 | Wikipedia article text, read raw |

Each instrument was control-tested before its results were believed — it had to
go red on a known-bad case in the same run, not merely green on good ones.

- **Designations.** The matcher confirms Comté PDO, rejects Comté claimed as
  IGP, refuses bare "Coppa" where only "Coppa di Parma" is registered, and
  returns nothing for a name absent from the register.
- **Latin names.** `Tuber melanosporumm` returns no match where
  `Crocus sativus` matches exactly.
- **Star claims.** Read from the raw article text, not a summary. This matters:
  on the first pass a summariser answered "2019" for Alchemist's stars where the
  article gives no year at all.

## Flagged, not changed

these is an editorial call about how the atlas names things.

*Queso Manchego* · Bresaola → *Bresaola della Valtellina* · Mortadella →

### Swiss, not EU (8) — correct, worth a note

Emmentaler, Gruyère, L'Etivaz, Sbrinz, Tête de Moine, Vacherin fribourgeois,
Raclette du Valais, viande des Grisons (*Bündnerfleisch*). All confirmed in the
Swiss federal register. They are a national scheme, not the EU one — and the EU
register's *Gruyère* PGI is the **French** cheese, a different product from
Swiss Gruyère AOP.

### Spirits — your call (6)

Armagnac, Cognac, Calvados, Marc de Bourgogne, Grappa, Brandy de Jerez carry
AOP or IGP badges. The EU register classes all six as spirit-drink
**geographical indications** (GI), not PDO/PGI. French usage calls Cognac and
Armagnac **AOC**. The badge vocabulary has no slot for either. I did not invent
one.

### Styles within an appellation — kept

Ruby / tawny / white port, fino, amontillado, oloroso, Pedro Ximénez, Marsala
dolce and secco, Madeira Malmsey and Sercial, Rivesaltes ambré, Asiago d'allevo,
Gorgonzola dolce and piccante. Each is a named type inside a registered
appellation — a tawny port is a Porto PDO wine. The badges stand.

### Latin names

889 of 1 335 matched the backbone exactly as currently accepted names. No bulk
edit was made, and none is recommended: the backbone's "synonym" list is mostly
its own lumping of infraspecific ranks (*Brassica oleracea* var. *italica* →
*Brassica oleracea*), and its accepted names for several kitchen staples —
*Lathyrus oleraceus* for the pea, *Salvia rosmarinus* for rosemary — would make
a cookery atlas worse, not better.

Worth a look, all small:

- *Schinus terebinthifolius* → *Schinus terebinthifolia* (pink peppercorn) —
  gender agreement; the backbone marks the corrected form ACCEPTED.
- *Ocimum basilicum* var. *thyrsiflora* → *thyrsiflorum* (Thai basil) — same.
- *Scapharca broughtonii* → *Anadara broughtonii* (akagai).
- *Lobatus gigas* → *Aliger gigas* (conch).
- *Morchella conica* → the backbone folds it into *M. esculenta*; the black
  morel's name is genuinely contested.
- *Tuber uncinatum* → *Tuber aestivum*; the burgundy truffle is now read as a
  form of the summer truffle.

**One caution about this instrument.** GBIF's matcher does not resolve
*Prunus dulcis* — the almond — falling back to the genus. So a `NONE` or
`HIGHERRANK` result is not evidence of an error, and the 351 such rows are
reported as inconclusive rather than as findings. Most of them are the atlas's
culinary Latin (*Butyrum clarificatum*, *Acetum vini*), which no taxonomic
backbone contains by design.

### Michelin stars

45 claims. **37 confirmed verbatim** against an opened page. **None contradicted.**

- Keller: the atlas gives 2006. Per Se took three stars in 2005, The French
  Laundry in 2006.
- Veyrat: 1995 and 2001 both confirmed. He took three stars a third time in
  2018 and lost them in the 2019 edition, then asked to leave the guide.
- Gagnaire: Saint-Étienne 1993 confirmed. The Paris year is not stated on the
  page.
- Unverified, no article found: Jordnær (Vildgaard), Piazza Duomo (Crippa),
  Den (Hasegawa), Sukiyabashi Jiro's years, André Pic.

The Michelin Guide's own site is unreachable from here — it refuses this
network at the CDN. Wikipedia article text was used instead, read raw.

### Origins

For the 106 products the EU register confirms, the register also names the
country. 28 atlas entries name a country outright in their origin line, and
**none contradicts the register**. The other 78 name a region instead —
"Roquefort-sur-Soulzon, Aveyron" rather than "France" — which is more precise,
not less, so no flag. The check was controlled: a planted "Somewhere in Italy"
against a France registration fires it.

### Price bands

Bands are internally coherent: medians rise 7.5 → 22.5 → 60 → 375 €/kg. Three
records sit outside their band — phycocyanine (band 3, sold by the gram, which
is band 4's own definition), benitade and labrador tea (band 2 at 150–300 €/kg,
where band 3's median is 60). The band is an editorial scale, so these are
listed, not changed.

## Not covered

The prose — origin stories, kitchen notes, dates inside them — is not checked.
Roughly 760 dated claims sit across 383 entries and each needs its own source.
That is the next pass, and it is the one that cannot be done from a register.
