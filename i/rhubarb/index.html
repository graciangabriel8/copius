Rhubarb

What it is

Botanically a vegetable — what we eat is a leaf stalk — and legally a fruit: the EU jam directive counts rhubarb stalks among the fruits, which is what lets rhubarb preserve be sold as jam. A US customs court had reached the same conclusion in 1947, on the ground that its chief use was that of a fruit. In Yorkshire’s “rhubarb triangle”, forced stalks grow in pitch-dark sheds and are harvested by candlelight; growers say you can hear them creak as they rise.

In the kitchen

Never the leaves — they are toxic. Roast the stalks rather than stewing them: they keep their shape, their colour, and their proud acidity.
