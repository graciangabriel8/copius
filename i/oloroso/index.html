What it is

Oloroso is fortified to seventeen degrees or more at the outset, above what the flor yeast can survive, so it never grows a veil and oxidises from its first year in cask. Oloroso means fragrant, and the confusion follows from there: it smells of raisin and walnut and is, in the classic style, bone dry — the sweet supermarket bottle is oloroso with syrup added afterwards.

In the kitchen

This is the sherry with body — glycerol and extract — so it thickens a braise where an amontillado would read thin. Reduce it with oxtail or beef-cheek liquor and resist adding sugar to chase the sweetness the nose promised.
