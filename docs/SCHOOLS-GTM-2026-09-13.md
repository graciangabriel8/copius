# Copius — how the first French school pays

**Decision document, 13 September 2026.** Every euro figure and named body traces to the verified research. Where the research said NOT FOUND, it says NOT FOUND.

## 1. THE ONE ROUTE

**Sell one annual whole-establishment licence to Lycée polyvalent hôtelier Le Renouveau (UAI 0422160V), signed by its own chef d'établissement, written into the OGEC's operating budget. Everything else waits behind that signature.**

It wins because it is the only route where the entire decision chain is people he can find in his own building this week, in the order they actually matter:

- **a teacher** who runs one séance with it (the "produits" slot in CAP 1re année is the obvious one),
- **the DDFPT** — the person whose documented job is advising the chef d'établissement on pedagogical equipment purchases and preparing the credit requests; Le Renouveau's DDFPT is listed as Yoann Quitaud on ANEPHOT's board page until its 12 September 2026 update,
- **the chef d'établissement**, who at a private school both proposes the budget and signs the spend within his OGEC delegation (FNOGEC statut-type 2015, delegations under article 139 du Statut de l'Enseignement catholique). Le Renouveau's is listed as Esther Milland, [his own direction] — *uncertain*, from an archived ANEPHOT board page; he knows the real name.

No procurement procedure applies at this amount on either side of the public/private line. Since 1 April 2026, article R2122-8 CCP lets a public buyer contract with no publicity and no competition below **EUR 60,000 HT** (décret n° 2025-1386 du 29 décembre 2025). **Important correction:** the argument that an OGEC is simply outside the Code de la commande publique because the forfait d'externat is not public financing **was refuted in fact-check** — that is not settled law, and article L1211-1 2° is satisfied by *any one* of three criteria, not all. Rely on the EUR 60,000 threshold, which holds regardless. Do not repeat the OGEC argument to anyone.

**Why it beats the alternatives, specifically:**

- **Publisher (BPI, Nathan, Bertrand-Lacoste).** Months of latency, no template — no French education publisher advertises licensing-in a finished third-party product from an individual. Royalties are low single digits shared across a team on a EUR 29.90 cover price, against EUR 18,600/yr kept in full from ~60 direct class licences. And the one reason it looked attractive — "droits d'auteur need no SIRET" — **was marked unverified in fact-check**, so the asymmetry may not exist.
- **GAR.** Closed to him outright: the June 2026 référentiel requires a *personne morale*, and a micro-entrepreneur is a personne physique. Irrelevant anyway — **zero** private lycées in the académie de Lyon are GAR-deployed, his own school included. And contract article 3 §15 would force him to distribute exclusively through GAR to every equipped school, surrendering the direct relationship.
- **Édu-Up.** Up to EUR 70,000, but it is a production grant, not revenue; next commission is 28 January 2027; and it too requires a personne morale (conflicting evidence — see §5).
- **AFLYHT partner slot.** EUR 2,600/year before a single customer exists. The charte admits suppliers by application to the conseil d'administration; a member school already using Copius is what makes that application land. Later, not now.
- **Public lycées.** Add the CA delegation question (R421-20 6° d), a budget proposal deadline of October 2026 for the 2027 year, and a Chorus Pro account. Same euros, more steps. Second customer, not first.

The first sale is not for the money. **EUR 300 is the unit; the asset is a named institution that paid.** Price it accordingly.

## 2. THE SEQUENCE

Copius is free and public at copius.fr. Any chef d'établissement will find that in one search. He must be able to say in one sentence what the school is buying that a teacher cannot get for free — a class licence with named teacher accounts, a printable bilingual set, a support commitment, an offline install on school machines, something. *Precondition for every other step.* If he cannot answer this, stop here; nothing downstream survives it.

| GAR adhesion | free, but all compliance costs on him; 2–3 months average | not now |

**Nothing in the route above forces him to register before first revenue.** Two things elsewhere do, and they change the vehicle, not just the timing: **GAR and Édu-Up both require a *personne morale*, and a micro-entreprise is not one.** That fork means an association loi 1901 (free, RNA number within 5 days, JOAFE publication free since 2019, SIREN on request) or a SASU. Decide that only if he actually wants Édu-Up or GAR; for selling to private schools, micro-entreprise is correct and simpler.

VAT franchise holds at **EUR 37,500** of services for 2026 — the single EUR 25,000 threshold was definitively abolished by the law of 3 November 2025. That is roughly 20 to 40 school contracts before it bites.

**Realistic calendar:** first euro in Q4 2026 at the earliest if the October conversation lands, and plausibly the 2027-28 school year if the OGEC budget is already closed. The Foxar precedent — two engineering students, first institutional buyer the Ville de Dijon in 2023 — took roughly five years from project to institutional purchase.

## 4. WHAT WOULD KILL IT

**1. "We already have BPI, and it's free."** This is the first objection and it is well-founded. Éditions BPI is ANEPHOT's official publishing partner; **BPI Campus is free** with 700+ videos and 300+ technical articles for cuisine, pastry, service and sommellerie, and BPI BOOKS bundles ~10,000 pages across 46 books. The earlier "no direct competitor" premise **does not survive the school channel**: Nathan publishes *Denrées et boissons* (2024, 368 p., 350+ products, EUR 29.90) and Delagrave publishes *Connaissance des denrées et des boissons* (Corinne Hacquemand, 2024) in STHR/BTS MHR and Bac Pro versions. The slot is occupied. He must read BPI Campus end to end and write one page on what Copius does that it does not, before pitching anyone.

**2. Free and beloved never converts.** Copius is free at copius.fr today. The two documented French precedents for exactly that shape are dead ends for revenue: La Digitale (35 apps, used nationally) runs on donations and needs a little over EUR 1,000/month for eleven servers; Sésamath (~80 volunteer teachers since 2001) earns only from an EUR 11 paper edition. The counter-precedent is Aude Guéneau — Plume was sold to her own colleagues and parents first, then labelled by the ministry in 2021, then raised EUR 2.2M. The difference is that she charged from the start. Step 0 exists because of this.

**3. He is invoicing the institution that grades him.** The chef d'établissement signs his stage conventions and his diploma paperwork. A "non" is free for them and expensive for him, and an awkward negotiation contaminates the rest of the year. Two mitigations: let the DDFPT carry the request internally rather than asking the direction cold, and do not bind the sale to his BTS **E32** project (*Projet d'entrepreneuriat*, coefficient 3, 20-page dossier at 60% plus a 15-minute oral at 40%) without his économie-gestion teacher agreeing in advance that a hospitality-sector *service* company fits the référentiel. If both the grade and the money ride on the same conversation, one bad answer takes both.
