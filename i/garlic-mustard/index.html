Garlic mustard

Alliaire officinale

FamilyHerbs
Season (France)March – May
Typical price (France)15–30 €/kg

What it is

Charred deposits on western Baltic pottery have yielded garlic mustard seed phytoliths dated to roughly 6,000 years ago, mixed with fish and deer fats — the earliest direct evidence in Europe of food seasoned for taste rather than for nourishment. The plant smells of garlic without being an allium at all: it is a crucifer, building that smell from sulphur compounds of its own.

In the kitchen

The garlic note is destroyed by heat, so chop it raw into a salsa verde or a soft butter. Take leaves before flowering — once it bolts they go hard and bitter — while the young seed pods stay usable as a pungent caper.
