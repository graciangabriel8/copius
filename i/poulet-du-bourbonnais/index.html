Bourbonnais chicken

Poulet du Bourbonnais

Latin nameGallus gallus domesticus (Bourbonnaise)
FamilyMeat & poultry
OriginBourbonnais, Allier
Season (France)All year
FlavourMeaty · Umami · Rich
Typical price (France)15–18 €/kg

What it is

Every bird is sired by a cock of the Bourbonnaise breed, recognised in 1919, and must show its white plumage with an ermine-marked neck. It is slaughtered at 101 days or more, after ranging on at least 6 m² a bird around wooden houses of 70 m² at most, and a final two to three weeks shut in on local cereals with 9 to 11% dairy and brewer's yeast; the specification links that finish to the fat in the thigh muscles and a taste it likens to chicken stock.

In the kitchen

The flesh is firm by design, so give it time rather than heat: roast it at 160 °C and rest it twenty minutes, or poach it whole and keep the liquid — stock is the specification's own word for its taste.
