What it is

Scholars suspect the “golden apples” of Greek myth were quinces — and the word marmalade comes from marmelo, Portuguese for quince, whose paste was the original marmalade. Inedibly hard and astringent raw, it performs the kitchen’s quietest miracle: hours of slow heat turn its pale flesh ruby.

In the kitchen

Poach it whole and slow with honey and saffron, or roast it beside lamb — its perfume forgives every hour it demands.
