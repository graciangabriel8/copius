Cornouaille cider

Cornouaille

Latin nameMalus domestica (cider apples)
FamilyWines & spirits
OriginSouth Finistère, 40 communes
Season (France)All year
FlavourBitter · Sweet · Fruity
Typical price (France)7–9 €/L

What it is

The juice must come from Breton varieties — Kermerrien, Marie Ménard, Guillevic, Dous Bloc'hig among them — with at least 70% bitter or bittersweet fruit, no more than 15% sharp, and no single variety above 60% of the blend. The must needs 108 g of natural sugar per litre, because nothing may be added: that is what leaves at least 20 g per litre after the bottle fermentation. Growers around Fouesnant sought the appellation after a 1987 decree let cider makers add concentrated juice.

In the kitchen

Use it in place of white wine to open mussels or to braise pork with apples; its residual sugar catches quickly on the pan, so keep the heat moderate once it goes in.
