Tamarillo

Latin nameSolanum betaceum
FamilyFruits
OriginAndes, now largely New Zealand
Season (France)June – October
FlavourSour · Tangy · Fruity
Typical price (France)10–20 €/kg

What it is

It was the tree tomato until 1 February 1967, when the New Zealand growers’ council adopted tamarillo — a coined word, part Maori tama, part Spanish amarillo — because the old name was costing them export sales. It remains a Solanum, and behaves like one: the skin is bitter and has to go, and the pulp reads savoury long before it reads dessert.

In the kitchen

Score a cross in the base and drop it into boiling water for thirty seconds; the bitter skin slips off exactly like a tomato’s. Salt it before you sugar it — it takes both, and salt tells you how much acid you are actually holding.
