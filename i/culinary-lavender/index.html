Culinary lavender

Lavande fine

Latin nameLavandula angustifolia
FamilyHerbs
OriginHigh Provence and the Alps
SeasonAll year
Flavourfloral · herbal · resinous
Typical price6–10 €/100 g

What it is

Lavender does not belong to traditional herbes de Provence — the flowers were added to the blend for the North American market in the twentieth century, and plenty of French cooks still leave them out. What the region actually grows for the kitchen is the narrow-leaved lavande fine of the high slopes, not the lavandin hybrid that carpets the postcard fields lower down.

In the kitchen

Use a quarter of what feels right: a teaspoon of buds infused in 250 g of hot cream for ten minutes, then strained, is already strong. Past ten minutes — or with a lavandin sold as lavender — it turns to soap.
