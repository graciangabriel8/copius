Century egg (pidan)

Œuf de cent ans (pidan)

Latin nameAnas platyrhynchos domesticus
FamilyDairy & eggs
OriginSouthern China
SeasonAll year
FlavourUmami · Pungent · Rich · Creamy
Typical price1–2 €/pièce

What it is

Nothing is aged for a century and nothing ferments: the egg is packed in a paste of lime, soda ash and salt that generates lye and drives the pH to somewhere between 9 and 12. At that alkalinity the proteins set without heat, which is the whole trick — the white turns to amber jelly, the yolk to grey-green cream, and the ammonia is only the reaction announcing itself.

In the kitchen

Chill it hard before peeling, then cut with a wet knife or a length of thread; a warm egg smears. A few minutes exposed to air lets the sharpest ammonia blow off — it wants vinegar, ginger and silken tofu, not salt.
