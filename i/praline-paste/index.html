Praline paste

Praliné

Latin nameCorylus avellana
FamilySweet
OriginMontargis and Lyon, France
SeasonAll year
Flavournutty · toasty · rich
Typical price20–40 €/kg

What it is

The name belongs to a seventeenth-century marshal, du Plessis-Praslin, whose cook is credited — the story goes — with dropping almonds into boiling sugar at Montargis. The paste came much later, when someone ground the cooled sweets, and it is that grinding rather than the caramel that made it a pastry ingredient.

In the kitchen

It separates in the tub like nut butter — stir the risen oil back in before you weigh, or the ganache is short of fat one day and swimming in it the next. Standard is fifty-fifty nuts to sugar; a sixty-forty runs looser and tastes far less sweet.
