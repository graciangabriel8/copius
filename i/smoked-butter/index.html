Smoked butter

Beurre fumé

FamilyDairy & eggs
Season (France)All year
Typical price (France)20–35 €/kg

What it is

The smoke has to be cold, under about 30 °C, or the butter simply melts and separates in the chamber, so producers either smoke the cream before churning or hold finished blocks over smouldering wood at low temperature. Smoking the cream gives the rounder result, because the fat takes up the phenols evenly, while a smoked block carries most of its flavour in the outer few millimetres.

In the kitchen

Melt it and spoon it over — do not fry in it, since the milk solids catch and the smoke turns to ash. It overwhelms delicate things: excellent on scallops, monkfish or beetroot, ruinous on scrambled eggs or a béchamel.
