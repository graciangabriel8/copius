Burgundy truffle ◆

Truffe de Bourgogne

Latin nameTuber uncinatum
FamilyMushrooms
OriginBeech and hazel woods, Burgundy and Lorraine
SeasonSeptember – January
Flavourearthy · nutty · musky · woody
Typical price300–600 €/kg

What it is

Genetically it is the same species as the summer truffle, and mycologists now write it Tuber aestivum uncinatum — but the trade has never merged the two, because a truffle lifted in November under beech is not the same object as one lifted in July. The autumn form has a dark, heavily marbled interior and a hazelnut-and-cocoa nose strong enough to hold through a minute of heat, which aestivum has not.

In the kitchen

Unlike the Périgord truffle it takes brief cooking, so it can go into a hot sauce at the very end rather than only raw over fat. Store it in a sealed jar with eggs, not buried in rice — rice pulls out the water its aroma depends on.
