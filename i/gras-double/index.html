What it is

The rumen is the first and largest of the four stomachs, and it leaves the tripier already blanched — flat, smooth, ivory, half-cooked before you have paid for it. Lyon cuts it into hand-sized flaps for tablier de sapeur, the crumbed square named for a leather apron and, the story goes, for Marshal de Castellane, military governor of the city and an unembarrassed eater of tripe.

In the kitchen

Even blanched it wants a good hour of simmering before anything else happens to it. Then marinate the flaps a few hours in white wine, mustard and lemon before crumbing — that acid is what stops the finished square tasting of the byre.
