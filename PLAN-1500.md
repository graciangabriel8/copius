## Candidates

### caviar-roe — 53

- **Adriatic sturgeon caviar** / Caviar naccarii — `caviar-naccarii` · seafood · rare  
  The Italian native sturgeon, nearly extinct in the wild and farmed only in the Po valley; small grey grain with a pronounced almond-milk sweetness unlike any other species.
- **Ankimo** / Foie de lotte (ankimo) — `foie-de-lotte` · seafood · luxe  
  Monkfish liver deveined, sake-soaked and steamed into a terrine; a dense rich organ product completely unlike the lean monkfish tail, and strictly a cold-season buy.
- **Baerii caviar** / Caviar baeri — `caviar-baeri` · seafood · luxe  
  Siberian sturgeon farmed in Aquitaine and Sologne; small dark grain, quick-maturing, buttery rather than nutty, and the workhorse French caviar bought by name for cooked and warm preparations.
- **Bafun uni** / Oursin bafun — `oursin-bafun` · seafood · both  
  A separate urchin species from the Mediterranean one already listed: small dense orange tongues with concentrated, almost caramelised umami, bought by name at the sushi counter.
- **Beluga caviar** / Caviar beluga — `caviar-beluga` · seafood · both  
  The largest grain of any caviar with a thin, almost bursting membrane and a mild creamy taste; CITES-restricted supply and price put it in a different buying channel from all other sturgeon roe.
- **Boquerones** / Boquerones (anchois marinés au vinaigre) — `boquerones` · seafood  
  White vinegar-cured anchovies, sharp and raw-tasting, with no fermentation or umami depth; they are a fresh acidic element, the opposite use to salted anchovy.
- **Cod liver** / Foie de morue — `foie-de-morue` · seafood · rare  
  Tinned cod liver cooks in its own oil into a soft, iodised, foie-gras-like paste; it is an offal luxury with its own texture and none of the flake of cod flesh.
- **Colatura di alici** / Colatura d'anchois — `colatura-d-anchois` · condiments · rare  
  The clear amber liquid drawn from Cetara's salt-maturing barrels: lighter, sweeter and far less funky than Southeast Asian fish sauce, and used raw and uncooked.
- **Conpoy** / Conpoy (Saint-Jacques séchées) — `conpoy` · seafood · luxe  
  Dried scallop adductor, shredded after soaking, delivering a caramel-sweet glutamate depth to broths and XO sauce that no fresh scallop can produce.
- **Dried abalone** / Ormeau séché — `ormeau-seche` · seafood · both  
  Sun-dried and aged for years, then braised for days into a gelatinous, deeply savoury whole piece; it is the single most expensive dried seafood and shares nothing with fresh abalone handling.
- **Dried oyster** / Huîtres séchées — `huitres-sechees` · seafood · rare  
  Sun-dried oysters turn dark, chewy and intensely smoky-sweet; they are braised whole in Cantonese festive dishes, a use no fresh oyster has.
- **Dried shrimp** / Crevettes séchées — `crevettes-sechees` · seafood  
  Salted, boiled and sun-dried shrimp used as a concentrated umami seasoning — soaked, pounded or fried — never as a shrimp substitute.
- **Fish maw** / Vessie natatoire séchée — `vessie-natatoire-sechee` · seafood · both  
  Dried swim bladder, almost pure collagen, that puffs or turns silky in braises and thickens soup by melting — bought by species and thickness, and priced like a luxury.
- **Herring milt** / Laitance de hareng — `laitance-de-hareng` · seafood  
  Soft roe, not eggs: a creamy male gonad that poaches or fries into a custardy texture and enriches sauces, the classic garnish for hareng à la meunière.
- **Ikura** / Œufs de saumon (ikura) — `ikura` · seafood  
  Large individually detached salmon eggs cured in soy and sake; the fatty, glossy burst and soy backbone make it a seasoning as much as a garnish, unlike any sturgeon roe.
- **Kaluga caviar** / Caviar kaluga — `caviar-kaluga` · seafood · luxe  
  The other Huso species: beluga-scale grain from the Amur basin, greenish-grey, with a buttery-marine profile and firmer shell than beluga, sold as its own product line.
- **Kazunoko** / Kazunoko (œufs de hareng) — `kazunoko` · seafood · rare  
  Herring roe kept as a whole cohesive slab prized for its crunch rather than its burst; desalted then marinated in dashi, it is a texture ingredient with no equivalent among loose roes.
- **Lobster coral** / Corail de homard — `corail-de-homard` · seafood · rare  
  The raw green roe of the hen lobster, which sets scarlet on heat; it is a thickener and colourant for bisque and beurre de corail, a role the meat cannot play.
- **Lumpfish roe** / Œufs de lump — `oeufs-de-lump` · seafood  
  Dyed black or red, firm, salty and pasteurised; used as a cheap saline punctuation and known to bleed colour, which is a handling constraint unique to it.
- **Masago** / Masago (œufs de capelan) — `masago` · seafood  
  Capelin roe is smaller and softer than tobiko with a mild bitterness; it disperses into sauces and mayonnaises instead of staying discrete, so the two are not interchangeable.
- **Matjes herring** / Hareng matjes — `hareng-matjes` · seafood · rare  
  Young pre-spawning herring ripened by its own enzymes in a light brine: sweet, soft, barely salty, with a short early-summer season and no smoke at all.
- **Mentaiko** / Mentaiko (œufs de colin marinés) — `mentaiko` · seafood · rare  
  Pollock roe sacs cured with chilli and kombu; scraped from the membrane it becomes a creamy piquant paste used as a sauce base, not a garnish.
- **Mojama** / Mojama (thon séché) — `mojama` · seafood · rare  
  Tuna loin salted and air-dried to a dense mahogany block that is shaved paper-thin — a cured meat in behaviour, closer to bresaola than to any tinned fish.
- **Murasaki uni** / Oursin murasaki — `oursin-murasaki` · seafood · both  
  The pale-yellow, larger, creamier Japanese urchin with a subtle wakame sweetness and an opposite season to bafun — chefs specify one or the other, never "uni".
- **Niboshi** / Niboshi (sardines séchées) — `niboshi` · condiments  
  Whole boiled-and-dried baby anchovies for dashi: bitter, oily and assertive where katsuobushi is delicate, and infused cold rather than flash-steeped.
- **Osetra caviar** / Caviar osciètre — `caviar-oscietre` · seafood · luxe  
  Russian sturgeon roe is a separate species from the generic caviar entry: large amber-to-brown grain, firm shell, nutty-walnut finish, and the standard reference caviar of French service.
- **Paddlefish caviar** / Caviar de polyodon — `caviar-polyodon` · seafood · rare  
  Not a sturgeon at all — the paddlefish is a separate family whose steel-grey grain is softer and earthier, and it carries its own legal EU designation "caviar de Polyodon".
- **Pressed caviar** / Caviar pressé (payusnaya) — `caviar-presse` · seafood · rare  
  Broken grains salted and pressed into a dense jam-like block; it is spread, sliced or melted into sauces rather than spooned, so it behaves like a completely different ingredient.
- **Sababushi** / Sababushi (maquereau séché) — `sababushi` · condiments · rare  
  Mackerel, not bonito: a darker, sweeter, higher-fat dried block whose dashi carries soba tsuyu and ramen where katsuobushi would be too fine.
- **Salt cod** / Morue salée — `morue-salee` · seafood  
  Heavily salted and pressed, it must be desalted for 24-48 hours before use and yields a fibrous, gelatinous flesh that fresh cod never gives.
- **Scallop coral** / Corail de Saint-Jacques — `corail-saint-jacques` · seafood  
  The orange gonad detached from the white muscle, iodised and slightly bitter; dried and powdered or blitzed into sauce, it is bought and priced separately from the noix.
- **Schrenckii caviar** / Caviar schrenckii — `caviar-schrenckii` · seafood · luxe  
  Amur sturgeon roe, the backbone of Chinese production: mid-size dark grain, high shell resistance, and a clean neutral taste chefs choose when the caviar must survive plating on a warm base.
- **Sea cucumber** / Holothurie séchée (concombre de mer) — `holothurie-sechee` · seafood · both  
  A dried echinoderm rehydrated over several days into a gelatinous, flavour-absorbing body wall; one of the four Chinese sea treasures and unlike any fish or shellfish.
- **Sevruga caviar** / Caviar sévruga — `caviar-sevruga` · seafood · luxe  
  Starry sturgeon gives the smallest, darkest grain with a markedly saline, iodine-forward flavour — chefs pick it precisely when osciètre would be too soft-spoken.
- **Shad roe** / Œufs d'alose — `oeufs-d-alose` · seafood · rare  
  Sold as whole paired lobes to be cooked — pan-fried in butter or bacon fat — rather than eaten raw; a brief spring river product with a liver-like, meaty texture.
- **Shiokara** / Shiokara — `shiokara` · condiments · rare  
  Squid flesh fermented in its own salted viscera into a pungent, viscous paste used by the spoonful as a seasoning — a fermented offal condiment, not a preserved seafood to be eaten in portions.
- **Shirako** / Shirako (laitance de morue) — `shirako` · seafood · rare  
  Cod milt served in convoluted brain-like lobes, eaten raw with ponzu or grilled; richer and far more fragile than herring milt, with a strict winter window.
- **Smoked cod roe** / Œufs de cabillaud fumés — `oeufs-de-cabillaud-fumes` · seafood  
  Whole smoked roe sac, dense and sliceable, that emulsifies into taramasalata — a smoke-driven ingredient with no raw-roe equivalent.
- **Smoked eel** / Anguille fumée — `anguille-fumee` · seafood · luxe  
  Hot-smoked eel is a finished ingredient of its own — dense, very fatty, sliceable — and behaves nothing like the fresh eel already listed, which needs cooking.
- **Smoked haddock** / Haddock (églefin fumé) — `haddock-fume` · seafood  
  In French kitchens "haddock" means the cured smoked fillet, poached in milk and flaked; the salt-and-smoke cure makes it a distinct pantry item, not a fresh fish.
- **Smoked herring** / Hareng saur — `hareng-saur` · seafood  
  Salted then cold-smoked whole, it is desalted in milk or oil and served in fillets — a pungent long-keeping product, not the fresh herring already listed.
- **Smoked salmon** / Saumon fumé — `saumon-fume` · seafood  
  Salted and cold-smoked, it is served raw in thin slices and keeps for weeks; salting has changed the protein so it can no longer be cooked like fresh salmon.
- **Smoked sturgeon** / Esturgeon fumé — `esturgeon-fume` · seafood · luxe  
  The flesh of the caviar fish, hot- or cold-smoked into firm ivory slices with a nutty fat; a by-product of caviar farms sold as its own charcuterie-style luxury.
- **Snail eggs** / Œufs d'escargot — `oeufs-d-escargot` · seafood · rare  
  White 4 mm pearls with a crunchy shell and an earthy, mushroom-and-humus taste; a land product entirely unlike fish roe, brined and sold semi-preserved.
- **Sterlet caviar** / Caviar sterlet — `caviar-sterlet` · seafood · rare  
  Smallest sturgeon, matured young; tiny fragile grain with a delicate, low-salt, almost hazelnut taste that collapses under heat, so it is used raw only.
- **Stockfish** / Stockfisch (morue séchée) — `stockfisch` · seafood · rare  
  Air-dried without salt on Norwegian racks until board-hard; rehydration takes days in running water and the result is chewier and sweeter than salt cod.
- **Sujiko** / Sujiko (œufs de saumon en poche) — `sujiko` · seafood · rare  
  Immature salmon roe salted while still in its membrane sac, sold as a sliceable block with a firmer, more concentrated taste — bought and handled quite differently from loose ikura.
- **Tobiko** / Tobiko (œufs de poisson volant) — `tobiko` · seafood  
  Flying-fish roe is tiny, crisp and audibly crunchy, holds colour and shape under dressing, and is used as a textural coating — a role no soft roe can fill.
- **Trout roe** / Œufs de truite — `oeufs-de-truite` · seafood  
  Small bright-orange grain that bursts sweet and low-salt; used for volume garnish and freshwater pairings where salmon roe would be too oily and caviar too costly.
- **Tuna bottarga** / Poutargue de thon — `poutargue-de-thon` · seafood · rare  
  Sicilian and Sardinian tuna roe is darker, meatier and far more assertive than mullet poutargue, and is shaved over robust dishes where mullet would disappear.
- **Vendace roe** / Œufs de corégone — `oeufs-de-coregone` · seafood · rare  
  The Kalix PDO roe of the Bothnian Bay: tiny pale-orange grain, brief autumn harvest, and a clean freshwater sweetness served with sour cream and red onion in its own Nordic idiom.
- **Ventresca** / Ventrèche de thon blanc — `ventreche-de-thon` · seafood · luxe  
  The albacore belly cut, hand-packed in olive oil: pale, gelatinous and far fattier than standard tinned tuna, bought by the cut and served intact.
- **White sturgeon caviar** / Caviar d'esturgeon blanc — `caviar-esturgeon-blanc` · seafood · luxe  
  California and Italian white sturgeon give a large glossy grain with a distinctly buttery, low-iodine flavour, bought as a named alternative to osciètre rather than as an origin of it.

### charcuterie — 49

- **Andouillette** / andouillette — `andouillette` · meat  
  Coarse chitterling and stomach strips packed lengthwise in a casing and sold raw to grill; pungent and offal-forward, unlike the smoked, sliced-cold andouille.
- **Bastourma** / bastourma — `bastourma` · meat · rare  
  Air-dried beef sealed in a thick çemen paste of fenugreek, garlic and paprika; the crust is eaten with the meat and makes it aromatic and bitter-warm, unlike any European dried beef.
- **Bigorre black ham** / jambon noir de Bigorre — `jambon-noir-de-bigorre` · meat · both  
  AOP pasture-raised Gascon black pig aged 20–36 months; the fat is soft and low-melting like ibérico rather than firm like white-pig ham, so it is warmed on the plate, not chilled.
- **Boudin blanc** / boudin blanc — `boudin-blanc` · meat  
  A pale emulsified sausage of pork or chicken forcemeat bound with milk and egg and gently poached — delicate and custardy, the opposite of blood sausage, and a festive-season item.
- **Bresaola** / bresaola — `bresaola` · meat  
  Valtellina beef eye of round cured in wine and spice and air-dried a few months, never smoked — bright, sour-edged and tender, dressed raw with lemon and oil.
- **Bündnerfleisch** / viande des Grisons — `viande-des-grisons` · meat  
  Alpine beef pressed repeatedly during drying into a dense rectangular block, unsmoked and far drier than bresaola; shaved across the grain into near-translucent squares.
- **Cecina de León** / cecina de León — `cecina-de-leon` · meat · luxe  
  Beef hind leg oak-smoked for two weeks then dry-cured over a year: smoky, dark, drier and much stronger than bresaola, and served with olive oil to compensate for its leanness.
- **Chorizo** / chorizo — `chorizo` · meat  
  Spanish cured sausage defined by pimentón, which colours the fat and bleeds orange into anything it is cooked with — a flavouring ingredient as much as a sliced salume.
- **Ciauscolo** / ciauscolo — `ciauscolo` · meat · rare  
  Marche salami ground two or three times and cured only about two weeks, leaving it spreadable — used on bread or stirred into sauces, not sliced.
- **Confit duck gizzards** / gésiers de canard confits — `gesiers-confits` · meat  
  Gizzard muscle confited until sliceable — dense, mineral and faintly chewy, warmed and fanned over salads; nothing else in the duck larder eats like it.
- **Coppa** / coppa — `coppa` · meat  
  Cured whole pork neck: a lace of intramuscular fat through lean muscle makes it sweeter, fattier and more melting than any cured loin, and it is the salume of choice under heat.
- **Cotechino** / cotechino — `cotechino` · meat  
  Raw boiling sausage built on pork rind, which dissolves into gelatin during hours of poaching to give a uniquely unctuous texture; a New Year item served with lentils.
- **Culatello di Zibello** / culatello de Zibello — `culatello-di-zibello` · meat · both  
  Only the boned rump heart of the leg, sewn into a pig bladder and aged in Po valley fog — no rind, no bone, no dry edge; sweeter and far moister than any prosciutto and traditionally rested in wine before slicing.
- **Cured goose breast** / jambon d'oie — `prosciutto-doca` · meat · rare  
  Goose breast salt-cured and air-dried in the Lomellina tradition: darker, leaner and more iron-scented than duck, with a thin fat cap that stays firm at room temperature.
- **Dried duck breast** / magret de canard séché — `magret-de-canard-seche` · meat  
  Whole fatted duck breast salt-cured and air-dried with its fat cap on; the fat stays soft and rendering-sweet, so slices are cut fat-side-through and often warmed.
- **Duck confit** / confit de canard — `confit-de-canard` · meat  
  Salt-cured duck leg cooked slowly in duck fat and stored submerged in it; bought ready-confited as the base of cassoulet, garbure and salads, and only crisped to order.
- **Figatellu** / figatellu — `figatellu` · meat · rare  
  Corsican fresh liver sausage of chestnut-fed pig, made only from December and eaten grilled over embers — liver-dominant, smoky and strictly a winter product.
- **Finocchiona** / finocchiona — `finocchiona` · meat  
  Tuscan salami dominated by wild fennel seed and flower, coarsely ground and deliberately kept soft and crumbly, so it is torn rather than cleanly sliced.
- **Goose fat** / graisse d'oie — `graisse-doie` · fats  
  Higher-melting and cleaner-tasting than duck fat, it crisps potatoes harder and stays firmer as a confit seal, with a milder flavour that does not mark delicate meats.
- **Goose foie gras** / foie gras d'oie — `foie-gras-doie` · meat · luxe  
  Paler, finer-grained and higher-melting than duck liver: it holds shape in a terrine and loses far less fat in the pan, with a milder, less iron-driven flavour.
- **Head cheese** / fromage de tête — `fromage-de-tete` · meat  
  Pig head meat simmered and set in its own gelatin with vinegar, shallot and parsley — a sliceable aspic terrine, bought whole and cut cold.
- **Ibérico shoulder** / paleta ibérique — `paleta-iberica` · meat · luxe  
  The foreleg, not the hind leg: smaller, higher fat-to-lean, shorter cure, more concentrated and chewy, and carved on a different bone geometry with a much lower usable yield.
- **Lap cheong** / saucisse chinoise — `saucisse-chinoise` · meat  
  Sweet cured pork sausage set with rose wine, soy and sugar, dried hard and always steamed or sliced into rice and stir-fries — never eaten raw.
- **Lardo di Colonnata** / lardo di Colonnata — `lardo-di-colonnata` · meat · luxe  
  Back fat cured in Carrara marble basins under a rosemary, garlic and spice brine, which keeps it wetter and far more aromatic than dry-salted lardo; it melts on contact with warm food.
- **Lomo embuchado** / lomo — `lomo-embuchado` · meat  
  Cured whole pork loin rubbed with pimentón and garlic — the leanest of the salumi, almost no marbling, so it is sliced paper-thin and never cooked.
- **Lonzu** / lonzu — `lonzu` · meat · rare  
  Corsican AOP cured pork loin from the chestnut-fed nustrale pig, dried with its fat cap intact — sweeter, fattier and more herbal than the lean Spanish lomo.
- **Lyon cooking sausage** / saucisson de Lyon à cuire — `saucisson-de-lyon-a-cuire` · meat · luxe  
  A raw, finely ground poaching sausage studded with pistachios and black truffle — cooked in broth or baked in brioche, never dried or eaten as-is.
- **Merguez** / merguez — `merguez` · meat  
  Fresh lamb and beef sausage in lamb casing, coloured and heated by harissa and paprika — no pork, no cure, grilled from raw and cooked fast.
- **Montbéliard sausage** / saucisse de Montbéliard — `saucisse-de-montbeliard` · meat  
  Narrower than Morteau, seasoned with caraway and cumin and only lightly smoked, so it is served in portions rather than sliced from a large sausage — a separate IGP with a separate use.
- **Morcilla de Burgos** / morcilla de Burgos — `morcilla-de-burgos` · meat  
  Blood sausage bulked with rice and sweet onion, so it crumbles into a savoury grain when heated instead of holding a slice like boudin noir.
- **Mortadella** / mortadelle — `mortadella` · meat  
  Finely emulsified pork studded with cubed neck fat, pistachio and myrtle berries, then slow steam-cooked — a cooked, sliceable emulsion, not a cured or dried salume.
- **Morteau sausage** / saucisse de Morteau — `saucisse-de-morteau` · meat  
  Wide Jura sausage cold-smoked 48 hours over resinous conifer sawdust and closed with a wooden peg; poached whole, it perfumes a whole pot with smoke.
- **Pancetta** / pancetta — `pancetta` · meat  
  Pork belly salt-cured with pepper and spice but never smoked — it renders clean and sweet, and is the correct fat for dishes where bacon smoke would be wrong.
- **Paris cooked ham** / jambon de Paris — `jambon-de-paris` · meat  
  Brine-cured and poached in a torchon rather than dried — mild, moist, unsmoked, sliced thick and warm; a different product class from every dry-cured ham.
- **Parsleyed Burgundy ham** / jambon persillé de Bourgogne — `jambon-persille` · meat  
  Cooked ham hock broken into chunks and set in a parsley, garlic and white-wine jelly; bought as a ready terrine, cut in cubes or slabs, and traditionally an Easter item.
- **Pastrami** / pastrami — `pastrami` · meat  
  Beef navel or brisket brined, crusted with coriander and cracked pepper, hot-smoked and then steamed to tenderness — served hot and hand-carved, unlike every air-dried beef.
- **Porchetta** / porchetta — `porchetta` · meat  
  Boned pork middle rolled around its own loin with fennel, garlic and rosemary and roasted to a shattering crackling, then sliced cold as charcuterie.
- **Pork rillettes** / rillettes — `rillettes` · meat  
  Pork slow-cooked in fat then shredded to fibres and packed under a fat seal — a spreadable, shelf-stable preparation rather than a sliced or whole-muscle product.
- **Prosciutto di San Daniele** / jambon de San Daniele — `prosciutto-di-san-daniele` · meat · luxe  
  Cured with the trotter still attached and pressed flat under weights in Friuli's dry alpine-Adriatic air, giving a sweeter, moister, less salty ham than the Emilian style, sliced thicker.
- **Salchichón** / salchichón — `salchichon` · meat  
  Spanish cured sausage seasoned with whole black peppercorns and no paprika — pale, peppery and nutty where chorizo is red and smoky.
- **Salt pork** / petit salé — `petit-sale` · meat  
  Belly, shoulder or ribs held in light brine only — raw, unsmoked and undried, it must be desalted then simmered, giving a soft boiled-meat texture no dry cure produces.
- **Serrano ham** / jambon serrano — `jamon-serrano` · meat  
  White-pig Spanish leg cured 9–18 months, lean and salt-forward rather than fat-marbled, so it is used as a cooking ham (wrapped, fried, in broths) where ibérico would be wasted.
- **Smoked cured pork belly** / poitrine fumée — `poitrine-fumee` · meat  
  Brine-cured belly cold- or hot-smoked over hardwood; the smoke is the flavour, and it is cut into lardons for garnishes where pancetta would read as too neutral.
- **Sobrasada** / sobrasada — `sobrasada` · meat  
  Mallorcan spreadable cured sausage of finely ground pork and paprika — soft enough to pipe or melt into sauces, sweeter and far less fiery than 'nduja.
- **Soppressata** / soppressata — `soppressata` · meat  
  Calabrian salami pressed flat under weights during curing and heavily spiked with chilli — the flattened section and heat set it apart from every round salame.
- **Speck Alto Adige** / speck — `speck-alto-adige` · meat  
  The only major cured leg that is both cold-smoked over beech and air-dried, boned and juniper-rubbed; the smoke means it is used cooked — in dumplings, sauces, risotto — as much as raw.
- **Stuffed duck neck** / cou de canard farci — `cou-de-canard-farci` · meat · luxe  
  Duck neck skin used as a casing for a forcemeat of duck, pork and foie gras, then confited — sliced cold as a terrine or seared; a Gascon preparation with no equivalent.
- **Toulouse sausage** / saucisse de Toulouse — `saucisse-de-toulouse` · meat  
  Fresh, uncured, coarsely hand-cut pork in a long coil — no nitrite, no drying; it must be cooked from raw and is the sausage cassoulet is built on.
- **Vendée ham** / jambon de Vendée — `jambon-de-vendee` · meat  
  Raw ham rubbed with eau-de-vie and a herb crust and cured only 8–12 weeks, so it stays soft, herbal and pale — carved in thick slices, never shaved like a long-aged ham.

### cheese — 84

- **Abondance** / Abondance — `abondance` · dairy · luxe  
  Haute-Savoie pressed uncooked wheel with a concave heel, softer and more lactic-fruity than Beaufort, and the cheese a berthoud is made with.
- **Appenzeller** / Appenzeller — `appenzeller` · dairy  
  Rind washed throughout affinage with a herbal wine-and-brine sulz, giving a spicy, almost fermented pungency no other alpine cheese has.
- **Asiago d'allevo** / Asiago d'allevo — `asiago-d-allevo` · dairy  
  The aged partially-skimmed type, hard and piquant, a grating cheese — a different product from the young full-fat Asiago pressato sold under the same DOP.
- **Banon** / Banon — `banon` · dairy · luxe  
  Wrapped in brown chestnut leaves tied with raffia and ripened inside them, which turns the paste unctuous and vegetal — the leaf is part of the ingredient.
- **Bitto** / Bitto — `bitto` · dairy · both  
  Made in Valtellina alpage huts from cow's milk with up to 10% Orobica goat milk, then aged for years to an intensely savoury, waxy paste.
- **Bleu d'Auvergne** / Bleu d'Auvergne — `bleu-d-auvergne` · dairy  
  Cow's-milk blue with fine dense veining and a wet, buttery paste; melts into sauces cleanly, which Roquefort's sheep fat does not.
- **Bleu de Gex Haut-Jura** / Bleu de Gex Haut-Jura — `bleu-de-gex` · dairy · rare

### fortified-wine — 50

- **Absinthe** / Absinthe — `absinthe` · condiments · rare  
  Distilled with grande wormwood, green anise and fennel at very high strength; its bitter herbal backbone, not just anise, is what defines oysters absinthe and certain shellfish sauces.
- **Amontillado** / Amontillado — `amontillado` · condiments · rare  
  A fino whose flor has died, then aged oxidatively, so it carries both yeast-derived and hazelnut oxidative notes at once; dry but deep, it is the sherry for consommé, jamón-based sauces and Ibérico pork.
- **Armagnac** / Armagnac — `armagnac` · condiments · luxe  
  Column-distilled once at low strength, so it keeps far more congeners than Cognac — rustic, prune-and-violet heavy, and the correct spirit for terrines, foie gras and Gascon prune desserts.
- **Banyuls** / Banyuls — `banyuls-wine` · condiments · rare  
  Coastal Roussillon vin doux naturel aged oxidatively in glass bonbonnes and old foudres, giving a rancio walnut-and-cocoa character used for duck, chocolate sauces and Roquefort — the wine, distinct from the vinegar already catalogued.
- **Brandy de Jerez** / Brandy de Jerez — `brandy-de-jerez` · condiments  
  Aged by solera in ex-sherry butts, so it arrives pre-loaded with oloroso and PX character; Spanish kitchens flambé and deglaze with it precisely for that sherry echo a Cognac cannot give.
- **Calvados** / Calvados — `calvados` · condiments  
  Apple (and in Domfrontais, pear) cider distillate; its orchard acidity is what makes the Norman trou normand, the tripes and the pork-and-cream sauces work, where a grape brandy would go flat.
- **Champagne vinegar** / Vinaigre de Champagne — `champagne-vinegar` · condiments  
  Made from Champagne base wine, it is markedly lower in acetic bite and more chalky-floral than ordinary white wine vinegar, which is why it dresses delicate leaves and raw fish where a standard vinegar would burn.
- **Cheongju** / Cheongju — `cheongju` · condiments · rare  
  Korean clear rice wine, less polished and slightly sweeter than sake, bought by name for marinades, galbi braises and jeon batter where its faint sweetness and odour-stripping action are expected.
- **Chinese rose wine** / Alcool de rose chinois (mei kuei lu) — `mei-kuei-lu-chiew` · condiments · rare

- **Cognac** / Cognac — `cognac` · condiments · luxe

- **Crème de cassis de Dijon** / Crème de cassis de Dijon — `creme-de-cassis` · condiments

- **Dark rum** / Rhum brun — `dark-rum` · condiments

- **Dry vermouth** / Vermouth sec (Noilly Prat) — `dry-vermouth` · condiments

- **Floc de Gascogne** / Floc de Gascogne — `floc-de-gascogne` · condiments · rare

- **Grappa** / Grappa — `grappa` · condiments

- **Junmai sake** / Saké junmai — `junmai-sake` · condiments

- **Malmsey Madeira** / Madère Malvoisie — `madeira-malmsey` · condiments · both

- **Manzanilla** / Manzanilla — `manzanilla-sherry` · condiments

- **Marsala dolce** / Marsala doux — `marsala-dolce` · condiments

- **Pineau des Charentes** / Pineau des Charentes — `pineau-des-charentes` · condiments  
  A mistelle of unfermented Charentais grape must muted with young Cognac, so it is grape sugar plus brandy rather than a fermented wine — sweet, fiery and used for melon, foie gras and shellfish sauces.
- **Raspberry eau-de-vie** / Eau-de-vie de framboise — `eau-de-vie-framboise` · condiments · luxe  
  Because raspberries carry almost no fermentable sugar, this takes many kilos per bottle and is among the costliest eaux-de-vie; it perfumes sorbets and soufflés without sweetening them, unlike crème de framboise.
- **Raspberry vinegar** / Vinaigre de framboise — `raspberry-vinegar` · condiments  
  Wine vinegar infused with raspberries until it turns fruity and pink, a genuine bought product with its own classic dishes — the nouvelle cuisine duck and calf's liver deglazings depend on its fruit, not just its acid.
- **Red verjuice** / Verjus rouge — `verjus-rouge` · condiments · rare  
  Pressed from unripe red grapes, it brings tannin and colour alongside the acidity, so it stains and grips where white verjus stays neutral — used for game, duck and red-fruit gastriques.
- **Rhum agricole** / Rhum agricole — `rhum-agricole` · condiments · rare  
  Distilled from fresh sugarcane juice rather than molasses, giving a grassy, olive-brine sharpness; a Martinique AOC product used where the cane character should show, and not interchangeable with dark rum in pastry.
- **Rivesaltes ambré** / Rivesaltes ambré — `rivesaltes-ambre` · condiments · rare  
  Made from white Grenache and Macabeu and aged two years in oxidative conditions, it carries apricot, hazelnut and candied-peel notes with no red-fruit weight, which makes it the white counterpart to Banyuls for poultry, foie gras and stone-fruit desserts.
- **Ruby port** / Porto ruby — `ruby-port` · condiments  
  Young, unoxidised and heavily fruited, it reduces to a purple, sweet, tannic glaze for game and figs — colour and grip are what it contributes, not nuttiness.
- **Sercial Madeira** / Madère Sercial — `madeira-sercial` · condiments · rare  
  The bone-dry Madeira style, high acid and saline after estufagem heating; it is the one used to lift consommé and clear meat jus without adding sugar.
- **Shanxi aged vinegar** / Vinaigre noir du Shanxi — `shanxi-vinegar` · condiments · rare  
  Solid-state fermented from sorghum, barley and pea, then sun-aged for years, it is drier, smokier and far more pungent than glutinous-rice Chinkiang, and northern kitchens use it on noodles and dumplings where Chinkiang would taste sweet.
- **Sweet red vermouth** / Vermouth rouge — `sweet-vermouth` · condiments  
  Caramelised, sweetened and spiced with cinchona and vanilla notes, it reduces to a dark bittersweet glaze for braised beef and game — the opposite culinary role to dry vermouth, which cannot substitute.
- **Tawny port** / Porto tawny — `tawny-port` · condiments · both  
  Long wood-aged and oxidised to walnut, caramel and dried apricot with the colour dropped out; it is the port for foie gras, veal jus and mushroom sauces, and behaves nothing like ruby.
- **Umeshu** / Umeshu — `umeshu` · condiments · rare  
  Green ume steeped in spirit and rock sugar, giving an almond-tinged sour-sweet liqueur; it is used to glaze pork and to build desserts, and differs from the non-alcoholic ume syrup in both acidity and depth.
- **Vin Santo** / Vin santo — `vin-santo` · condiments · rare  
  Tuscan appassimento wine aged for years in sealed small caratelli with a solera-like madre, oxidative and nutty where vin de paille stays fresh and fruity; it is the deglazing liquid for chicken liver crostini and the soak for cantucci.
- **Vin de paille** / Vin de paille — `vin-de-paille` · condiments · both  
  Grapes dried on straw for months before pressing, producing a low-volume raisined sweet wine used to deglaze foie gras and glaze fruit — a different product from any fortified sweet wine because nothing is added, the sugar is concentrated by drying.
- **Vin jaune** / Vin jaune — `vin-jaune` · condiments · both  
  Savagnin aged six years under a yeast veil without topping up, giving a curry-walnut oxidative wine that behaves like nothing else in a sauce — it is the mandatory liquid for poulet au vin jaune and morilles, not interchangeable with any dry white.
- **Vincotto** / Vincotto — `vincotto` · sweet · rare  
  Puglian must from Negroamaro and Primitivo reduced and then aged in wood, often with an acetic edge; darker, more tannic and less caramel-sweet than Emilian saba, and used as a finishing condiment on meat and cheese.
- **White port** / Porto blanc — `white-port` · condiments  
  Fortified from white Douro grapes, dry to off-dry and citric rather than red-fruited; it deglazes fish, chicken livers and shellfish where any red port would stain and clash.

### game-poultry — 48

- **Akaushi wagyu** / Wagyu Akaushi (japonais brun) — `wagyu-akaushi` · meat · luxe  
  Japanese Brown rather than Japanese Black: markedly leaner, with beefier red-meat flavour instead of buttery richness, so it can be cut and served as a full portion where A5 Kuroge cannot.
- **Aubrac beef** / Bœuf Aubrac — `boeuf-aubrac` · meat · luxe  
  Hardy highland breed whose late-maturing carcass carries fine intramuscular marbling and firm, deep-yellow fat; it is the French breed bought specifically for long dry ageing.
- **Bison** / Bison — `bison` · meat  
  A separate bovine genus from cattle — leaner, sweeter, coarser-fibred meat that cooks roughly a third faster than beef at the same thickness.
- **Blonde d'Aquitaine beef** / Bœuf Blonde d'Aquitaine — `boeuf-blonde-aquitaine` · meat  
  Very lean, large-muscled South-West breed with pale fat and low marbling — the beef chosen for tartare, carpaccio and thin cuts, and the one that overcooks fastest.
- **Boiling fowl** / Poule (à bouillir) — `poule-a-bouillir` · meat  
  A retired laying hen bought for its bones and skin rather than its meat: the only bird that yields a properly gelatinous consommé or poule au pot broth, where a roasting chicken gives a thin one.
- **Charolais beef** / Bœuf Charolais — `boeuf-charolais` · meat  
  Heavy Burgundian breed with white fat and broad, coarse-grained muscles that hold up to braising and pot-au-feu where finer breeds fall apart.
- **Chianina beef** / Bœuf Chianina — `boeuf-chianina` · meat · luxe  
  Giant white Tuscan breed with very lean, large-diameter loin muscles — the only carcass that yields a true five-centimetre bistecca alla fiorentina, and it is served bleu by necessity.
- **Fallow deer** / Daim — `daim` · meat  
  A separate deer species, mostly park-farmed, with mild, evenly marbled meat and a larger loin than roe deer — the deer used when a menu wants venison without the wild edge.
- **Grey partridge** / Perdreau gris — `perdreau-gris` · meat · both  
  The plains partridge Escoffier ranked above the red-legged for finesse: smaller, paler-fleshed, subtler, and roasted very briefly where the red bird is braised.
- **Hind** / Biche — `biche` · meat · luxe  
  The female red deer sold to kitchens by name because it carries none of the rut musk of a stag; finer-grained and paler, it is roasted where stag meat is braised.
- **Iberian pork (bellota)** / Porc ibérique de bellota — `porc-iberique-bellota` · meat · luxe  
  Fresh meat from acorn-finished Iberian pigs: dark red, oleic-fat-marbled and served pink like beef, a different raw material from the cured ham already listed and from white pork.
- **Kid goat** / Chevreau — `chevreau` · meat · both  
  Milk-fed kid of six to twelve weeks, leaner and cleaner-tasting than lamb with almost no fat cover, so it is braised or slow-roasted rather than grilled.
- **Kintoa pork** / Porc Kintoa — `porc-kintoa` · meat · rare  
  AOP meat from the Pie noir du Pays basque breed reared on Basque hillsides, softer-fatted and sweeter than Noir de Bigorre, and a genuinely separate breed and appellation.
- **Kobe beef** / Bœuf de Kobe (Tajima) — `boeuf-de-kobe` · meat · luxe  
  Tajima-strain Japanese Black certified in Hyōgo, with a fat melting point low enough that it renders at hand heat; it is served in a few thin slices, not as a steak, unlike broader wagyu.
- **Limousin beef** / Bœuf Limousin — `boeuf-limousin` · meat  
  Fine-fibred, thin-skinned breed with a high lean yield and little cover fat; roasts and quick-grilled cuts, not a breed for long ageing or braising.
- **Mallard** / Canard colvert — `canard-colvert` · meat · both  
  Wild-shot duck with dark, iron-tasting, near-fatless breast that must be served rare; nothing about it behaves like the farmed ducks bred from the same species.
- **Mangalitsa pork** / Porc mangalitza — `porc-mangalitza` · meat · rare  
  Woolly Hungarian lard breed carrying two to three times the back fat of a modern pig, with soft unsaturated fat; bought for that fat as much as for the meat.
- **Milk-fed veal** / Veau sous la mère — `veau-sous-la-mere` · meat · luxe  
  Calf suckled at the dam and never given feed, giving near-white, faintly sweet flesh that must be cooked gently and pale; standard rosé veal is redder and firmer.
- **Mulard duck** / Canard mulard — `canard-mulard` · meat · luxe  
  Sterile Barbarie × Pékin hybrid raised only for gavage; its carcass after the liver is taken is the source of magret, confit legs and duck fat, and the flesh is far fattier and redder than a table duck's.
- **Muscovy duck** / Canard de Barbarie — `canard-de-barbarie` · meat  
  A different species from the mallard-descended table ducks — larger, much leaner, deep red meat with almost no subcutaneous fat, so it roasts closer to game than to duck.
- **Mutton** / Mouton — `mouton` · meat  
  Sheep over two years: dark, densely flavoured, tallowy meat that needs long moist cooking and is the correct animal for a navarin or a curry where lamb goes bland.
- **Noir de Bigorre pork** / Porc noir de Bigorre — `porc-noir-de-bigorre` · meat · luxe  
  AOP meat from the pure Gascon black pig, aged twelve months or more on Pyrenean pasture and acorns; dark, dense, intramuscular-fatted flesh unlike the Basque Kintoa or Iberian pig.
- **Pauillac lamb** / Agneau de Pauillac — `agneau-de-pauillac` · meat · luxe  
  Unweaned milk lamb of 11–15 kg killed at about 75 days, with near-white flesh and no forage in the diet; delicate to the point that ordinary lamb seasoning overwhelms it.
- **Pekin duck** / Canard de Pékin — `canard-de-pekin` · meat  
  Thin-skinned, thick-fat-layered bird bred so the skin separates and crisps under lacquer and hanging-drying — a structural property no French table duck has.
- **Pluma ibérica** / Pluma ibérique — `pluma-iberique` · cuts · luxe  
  The triangular tip of the loin at the shoulder end, feather-shaped and finely marbled; the leanest of the Iberian grilling cuts and the one served rarest.
- **Poularde** / Poularde — `poularde` · meat · luxe  
  A young hen held back from laying and fattened to 2 kg of pale, fat-laced flesh, poached whole and finished in cream rather than roasted like a chicken, and female where a capon is a castrated male.
- **Poultry oyster** / Sot-l'y-laisse — `sot-l-y-laisse` · cuts · rare  
  The two walnut-sized muscles in the pelvic hollow of a bird, sold trimmed by the kilo from turkey and chicken carcasses; the most tender meat on any fowl and cooked on its own as a garnish.
- **Presa ibérica** / Presa ibérique — `presa-iberique` · cuts · luxe  
  A thick disc of shoulder muscle above the blade, richly veined and dense enough to roast whole and rest like a beef fillet, unlike the thin secreto and pluma.
- **Ptarmigan** / Lagopède — `lagopede` · meat · rare  
  Nordic mountain bird imported frozen for winter menus; paler and milder than grouse with a birch-and-lichen note, and it dries out at the point grouse is still rare.
- **Red grouse** / Grouse d'Écosse — `grouse-ecosse` · meat · luxe  
  Heather-fed Scottish bird with almost bitter, peaty dark flesh unlike any French game bird, and its 12 August opening makes it the earliest game of the year.
- **Red-legged partridge** / Perdrix rouge — `perdrix-rouge` · meat · luxe  
  A different species from the grey partridge — larger, darker, firmer and more strongly flavoured, and an adult bird needs a casserole or terrine rather than a roast.
- **Reindeer** / Renne — `renne` · meat · rare  
  Lichen-fed Nordic meat with a sweet, faintly resinous flavour and almost no intramuscular fat; sold as loin or cold-smoked, and it is grey and dry past rare.
- **Roe deer** / Chevreuil — `chevreuil` · meat · luxe  
  A different species from red deer: a small carcass with fine-grained, barely gamey meat that is roasted pink in minutes and ruined by the marinade red-deer venison expects.
- **Rooster** / Coq — `coq` · meat  
  A mature male bird with dark, sinewy, collagen-heavy flesh that is inedible roasted and only becomes food after hours of wine braising — a different cooking regime from any table chicken.
- **Rouge des Prés beef** / Bœuf Rouge des Prés (Maine-Anjou) — `boeuf-rouge-des-pres` · meat · luxe  
  The only French beef with an AOC, from cull cows finished on Loire valley grass; marbled and yellow-fatted like an old-cow beef but at a French carcass size.
- **Rubia Gallega old-cow beef** / Bœuf Rubia Gallega (vaca vieja) — `boeuf-rubia-gallega` · meat · luxe  
  Galician cows taken at eight to fourteen years, giving deep purple meat and thick buttercup-yellow fat with a blue-cheese note; cut as a thick bone-in chuleta and never trimmed of that fat.
- **Salers beef** / Bœuf Salers — `boeuf-salers` · meat · luxe  
  Cantal breed usually taken from cull cows rather than young bulls, giving dark, close-grained, strongly mineral meat that suits a thick grilled côte rather than a fillet.
- **Salt marsh lamb** / Agneau de pré-salé — `agneau-de-pre-sale` · meat · luxe  
  AOP lamb grazed on halophyte marshes of the Mont-Saint-Michel and Somme bays, which puts a genuine iodine and samphire note into the fat — it needs no salt and no strong garnish.
- **Secreto ibérico** / Secreto ibérique — `secreto-iberique` · cuts · luxe  
  A thin fan of heavily marbled muscle hidden under the shoulder fat, about 200 g per pig; grilled hard and fast over charcoal, it behaves like a fatty steak, not like any pork cut.
- **Sisteron lamb** / Agneau de Sisteron — `agneau-de-sisteron` · meat · luxe  
  Provençal IGP lamb from Mérinos d'Arles and Préalpes ewes, weaned and grass-finished on garrigue, so it carries herb-scented flavour absent from the milk lambs of Pauillac.
- **Snipe** / Bécassine des marais — `becassine` · meat · rare  
  A marsh bird a quarter the size of woodcock, sweeter and muddier in flavour, cooked two or three per plate in minutes rather than one bird per person.
- **Spider steak** / Araignée — `araignee` · cuts · rare  
  A single web-veined muscle off the hip socket, roughly 500 g per animal, loose-grained and juicy; a butcher's cut that never reaches a counter and is grilled rare and sliced across.
- **Suckling pig** / Cochon de lait — `cochon-de-lait` · meat · luxe  
  An unweaned piglet of four to six weeks with gelatinous, undeveloped flesh and paper-thin skin that blisters to glass; roasted whole and boned, it shares no technique with adult pork.
- **Teal** / Sarcelle — `sarcelle` · meat · rare  
  The smallest wild duck, one bird per person, roasted whole in eight to ten minutes; delicate and faintly saline where mallard is heavy and ferrous.
- **Wild rabbit** / Lapin de garenne — `lapin-de-garenne` · meat · both  
  Shot rather than farmed: half the weight of a hutch rabbit, dark and herb-scented flesh with almost no fat, and only the saddle is worth roasting.
- **Wood pigeon** / Palombe — `palombe` · meat · both  
  Wild migratory pigeon of the South-West, dark and strongly gamey with a firmer breast than farmed squab, and tied to the brief October palombière season.
- **Woodcock** / Bécasse — `becasse` · meat · both  
  The one game bird roasted undrawn with its trail served on toast; intensely liver-scented dark flesh that no other feathered game approaches, and it must be shot rather than farmed.
- **Young wild boar** / Marcassin — `marcassin` · meat · luxe  
  A boar under six months, still striped: pale, tender, mild meat that is roasted straight, where adult wild boar needs days of marinade to be palatable.

- **Culinary lime (cal)** / Chaux alimentaire — `chaux-alimentaire` · grains · rare

- **Diastatic malt powder** / Malt diastasique — `malt-diastasique` · grains

- **Edible gold leaf** / Feuille d'or alimentaire — `feuille-d-or` · condiments · luxe

- **Edible silver leaf** / Feuille d'argent alimentaire — `feuille-d-argent` · condiments · luxe

- **Egg white powder (albumin)** / Blanc d'œuf en poudre — `blanc-d-oeuf-en-poudre` · sweet

- **Feuilletine** / Pailleté feuilletine — `paillete-feuilletine` · sweet  
  Crushed thin crêpe dentelle bought by the kilo purely for a crackling texture that survives being bound in chocolate or praliné — a structural crunch, not a biscuit.
- **Fish gelatine** / Gélatine de poisson — `gelatine-de-poisson` · sweet · rare

- **Glucono delta-lactone** / Glucono-delta-lactone — `glucono-delta-lactone` · condiments · rare

- **Guar gum** / Gomme de guar — `gomme-de-guar` · condiments

- **Gum arabic** / Gomme arabique — `gomme-arabique` · condiments  
  Acacia exudate that dissolves at very high concentration without thickening much, which makes it the film-former for shine on chocolate and pastillage and the emulsifier for flavour oils in syrups.
- **Gum tragacanth** / Gomme adragante — `gomme-adragante` · sweet · rare  
  The only gum that turns sugar paste into a plastic, sculptable, air-drying mass, which is why pastillage and sugar flowers are made with it and with nothing else.
- **High-acyl gellan gum** / Gomme gellane haute acyle — `gomme-gellane-haute-acyle` · condiments

- **Hydrolysed soy protein (Versawhip)** / Protéine de soja hydrolysée — `proteine-de-soja-hydrolysee` · condiments · rare

- **Instant modified waxy maize starch** / Amidon de maïs cireux modifié — `amidon-de-mais-cireux-modifie` · condiments · rare

- **Iota carrageenan** / Carraghénane iota — `carraghenane-iota` · condiments

- **Kansui** / Kansui — `kansui` · grains · rare  
  An alkaline solution of sodium and potassium carbonates that turns wheat dough yellow, springy and slippery; it is what makes a ramen noodle a ramen noodle rather than a pasta.
- **Kappa carrageenan** / Carraghénane kappa — `carraghenane-kappa` · condiments  
  A refined seaweed extract that sets a firm, brittle, slightly syneretic gel in the presence of potassium; it is a standardised powder dosed to the tenth of a gram, unlike the whole Irish moss already listed.
- **Koji spores (tane-koji)** / Spores de koji — `spores-de-koji` · condiments · rare  
  The living mould inoculum, sold by strain and spore count, from which koji is grown — the raw material behind the finished koji rice already listed, not a version of it.
- **Konjac glucomannan** / Glucomannane de konjac — `glucomannane-de-konjac` · condiments

- **Lactic acid** / Acide lactique — `acide-lactique` · condiments  
  A soft, round, dairy-like sourness with no citrus edge, used to acidify without brightening — the acid for fermented flavours and for adjusting butter sauces.
- **Lambda carrageenan** / Carraghénane lambda — `carraghenane-lambda` · condiments · rare  
  The non-gelling carrageenan: it only thickens, and does so in cold dairy, which makes it the cream and milk body-builder where kappa and iota would set the liquid solid.
- **Leaf gelatine** / Gélatine en feuilles — `gelatine-en-feuilles` · sweet

- **Locust bean gum** / Gomme de caroube — `gomme-de-caroube` · condiments

- **Low-acyl gellan gum** / Gomme gellane basse acyle — `gomme-gellane-basse-acyle` · condiments

- **Malic acid** / Acide malique — `acide-malique` · condiments

- **Methylcellulose A4M** / Méthylcellulose A4M — `methylcellulose-a4m` · condiments · rare

- **Methylcellulose F50 (HPMC)** / Méthylcellulose F50 — `methylcellulose-f50` · condiments · rare

- **Nigari** / Nigari — `nigari` · condiments · rare

- **Pectin NH** / Pectine NH — `pectine-nh` · sweet

- **Pectin X58** / Pectine X58 — `pectine-x58` · sweet · rare

- **Pectinase** / Pectinase — `pectinase` · condiments · rare

- **Phycocyanin** / Phycocyanine — `phycocyanine` · condiments · rare

- **Rapid-set apple pectin** / Pectine de pomme à prise rapide — `pectine-de-pomme-prise-rapide` · sweet

- **Rennet** / Présure — `presure` · dairy

- **Skimmed milk powder** / Lait écrémé en poudre — `lait-ecreme-en-poudre` · dairy  
  Bought to raise the non-fat milk solids of an ice-cream base, which binds free water and controls ice crystals — a texture lever fresh milk cannot pull without adding water.
- **Sodium alginate** / Alginate de sodium — `alginate-de-sodium` · condiments

- **Sodium bicarbonate** / Bicarbonate de sodium — `bicarbonate-de-sodium` · sweet

- **Sodium citrate** / Citrate de sodium — `citrate-de-sodium` · condiments

- **Sorbitol** / Sorbitol — `sorbitol` · sweet

- **Soy lecithin** / Lécithine de soja — `lecithine-de-soja` · condiments

- **Sucrose ester** / Sucroester — `sucroester` · condiments · rare

- **Sunflower lecithin** / Lécithine de tournesol — `lecithine-de-tournesol` · condiments  
  Bought specifically because it is allergen-free and completely flavourless, so it can foam delicate infusions and citrus juices where soy lecithin's taste would show.
- **Tapioca maltodextrin** / Maltodextrine de tapioca — `maltodextrine-de-tapioca` · condiments

- **Tartaric acid** / Acide tartrique — `acide-tartrique` · condiments

- **Transglutaminase** / Transglutaminase — `transglutaminase` · condiments · rare

- **Trehalose** / Tréhalose — `trehalose` · sweet · rare

- **Vegetable glycerine** / Glycérine végétale — `glycerine-vegetale` · sweet

  Ground blanched kernel is a separate pastry commodity from the whole nut: it carries roughly 60% fat into a batter, changing crumb and browning in ways almond powder does not, and is bought and stocked as its own line.
- **Japanese chestnut** / Châtaigne japonaise (kuri) — `japanese-chestnut` · nuts · rare  
  A different species: much larger, starchier and less sweet, with an inner shibukawa skin that clings and must be simmered off over days — the handling and the dishes (kuri gohan, kuri kinton, shibukawa-ni) have no European equivalent.
- **Kola nut** / Noix de kola — `kola-nut` · nuts · rare  
  Bitter, caffeinated and astringent, used grated or infused as a bittering aromatic in West African cooking and in chocolate and bar work — a flavouring seed, never a nut eaten for its fat.
- **Korean pine nut** / Pignon de pin de Corée — `korean-pine-nut` · nuts  
  The small triangular Northeast Asian kernel is milder and softer than Mediterranean stone pine, and is the pine nut of Korean and Chinese cooking — jatjuk, pine-nut oil garnish — bought by species precisely to avoid the Pinus armandii kernels that cause pine mouth.
- **Largueta almond** / Amande Largueta — `largueta-almond` · nuts  
  The long, lean Spanish kernel is lower in fat and slightly bitter, staying crunchy through roasting and enrobing — the almond for peladillas, garrapiñadas and chocolate work where Marcona would go greasy.
- **Lotus seed** / Graine de lotus — `lotus-seed` · nuts  
  Fresh it is crisp and cucumber-sweet, eaten raw with the bitter green germ removed; dried it cooks to a floury chestnut texture for soups and tong sui. A separate ingredient from the lotus root and leaf already catalogued.
- **Lotus seed paste** / Pâte de graines de lotus — `lotus-seed-paste` · sweet  
  Dried seeds cooked, milled and fried down with sugar and oil into a dense, faintly floral paste graded by white or yellow seed; the standard mooncake and bao filling, bought finished and behaving nothing like the seed.
- **Marcona almond** / Amande Marcona — `marcona-almond` · nuts · luxe  
  A round, very high-fat kernel that fries soft rather than crisp and reads closer to macadamia than to almond — used fried and salted or in picada, and never a substitute for a baking almond.
- **Marzipan** / Massepain — `marzipan` · sweet  
  A cooked or milled almond-and-sugar paste bought at a stated almond ratio (from 33% up to Lübeck's 70%+); it is a modelling and layering material with its own plasticity, not almonds plus sugar assembled in the kitchen.
- **Orgeat syrup** / Sirop d'orgeat — `orgeat-syrup` · sweet  
  An emulsified almond syrup carrying orange-flower water and bitter-almond aroma; it behaves as a liquid sweetener with body, used in cocktails, granita and soaking syrups where almond paste or milk cannot go.
- **Perilla seed** / Graine de périlla — `perilla-seed` · nuts · rare  
  Deulkkae is toasted and ground into Korean soups and namul as a thickening, aromatic powder with a distinct grassy-nutty smell; the whole seed and its powder are separate purchases from the perilla oil pressed from it.
- **Persipan** / Persipan — `persipan` · sweet · rare  
  A legally defined paste made from debittered apricot or peach kernels instead of almonds, with a sharper stone-fruit bitterness — used in German and Austrian baking where marzipan would read too sweet, and labelled separately by law.
- **Piedmont hazelnut** / Noisette du Piémont — `piedmont-hazelnut` · nuts · luxe  
  The Tonda Gentile roasts to a clean-peeling, intensely aromatic kernel with no astringent skin residue, which is why it is the reference nut for gianduja and praline paste rather than an interchangeable hazelnut.
- **Pili nut** / Noix de pili — `pili-nut` · nuts · rare  
  A Bicol volcanic-soil kernel with the highest fat of any commercial nut, roasting to a texture softer than macadamia and melting rather than crunching; the shell is hard enough that it is only ever sold shelled, at a premium.
- **Pinyon pine nut** / Pignon pinyon — `pinyon-pine-nut` · nuts · rare  
  Wild-gathered in the American Southwest, tiny, thin-shelled and openly resinous-sweet where stone pine is buttery; a masting crop with no reliable annual supply, sold at multiples of Mediterranean pine nut prices.
- **Pistachio paste** / Pâte de pistache — `pistachio-paste` · sweet · luxe  
  A 100% ground-kernel paste, not a flavouring: it is a pastry base ingredient with its own fat behaviour in creams, ice cream and ganache, bought by kernel origin rather than as a nut.
- **Périgord walnut** / Noix du Périgord — `perigord-walnut` · nuts · luxe  
  A four-cultivar AOP — Marbot, Corne, Grandjean, Franquette — each used differently: Marbot eaten fresh, Corne pressed for its dark, deeply roasted-tasting oil. A composite product, not a synonym for Grenoble.
- **Sacha inchi** / Sacha inchi — `sacha-inchi` · nuts · rare  
  An Amazonian star-pod seed, toxic raw and roasted to a green, faintly grassy nut with unusually high alpha-linolenic acid; used whole and pressed in Peruvian high-end cooking, and unstable to heat once pressed.
- **Tiger nut** / Souchet — `tiger-nut` · nuts  
  A sedge tuber, not a nut: dense, sweet and fibrous, soaked and pressed for horchata de chufa or milled into a flour that behaves like a starch. Nut-free by botany, which is exactly why kitchens reach for it.
- **Tombul hazelnut** / Noisette Tombul (de Giresun) — `tombul-hazelnut` · nuts  
  The round Black Sea cultivar is the volume benchmark chefs specify by name for roasting and nut butters — thinner-skinned and sweeter than Piedmont but without its aromatic depth, and priced for use in quantity.
- **Water caltrop** / Mâcre — `water-caltrop` · nuts · rare  
  The horned aquatic seed sold as ling jiao in Chinese markets — starchy, chestnut-like and eaten boiled or braised. A completely different plant from the Eleocharis water chestnut, which is a crisp corm eaten raw.
- **Wattleseed** / Graines d'acacia — `wattleseed` · nuts · rare  
  Roasted and ground Australian acacia seed used as an aromatic rather than a nut: coffee, cocoa and hazelnut notes with a bitter edge, infused into custards and ice cream the way vanilla or coffee would be.
- **White poppy seed** / Pavot blanc — `white-poppy-seed` · nuts  
  Khus khus is used ground as a thickener and body-builder in Indian korma and Bengali posto, contributing texture and near no flavour — the opposite job from blue poppy seed, which is used whole for its nutty crunch and oil.

### offal — 58

- **Abura-kasu** / Abura-kasu — `abura-kasu` · cuts · rare  
  Beef small intestine confited slowly in its own fat until it dehydrates into crisp nuggets with a gelatinous core — a shelf-stable pantry ingredient scattered over udon or okonomiyaki, not fresh offal.
- **Amourettes (spinal marrow)** / Amourettes — `amourettes` · cuts · rare  
  Spinal cord, not bone marrow: a long soft rope that is peeled, poached and cut into batons for fritots and financière garnish, and EU specified-risk-material rules now confine it to veal under twelve months and to lamb.
- **Beef tendon** / Tendon de bœuf (nerf) — `tendon-de-boeuf` · cuts  
  Pure collagen that is inedible until hours of moist heat turn it translucent and slippery; sold trimmed by the kilo for pho, gyu-suji and Cantonese braises, and never a substitute for any muscle.
- **Bible tripe** / Feuillet — `feuillet` · cuts · rare  
  The third stomach, made of hundreds of thin leaves; it is used for crunch rather than for melt — barely dipped in hotpot as Chinese maodu or Korean cheonyeop — the opposite cooking to every other tripe.
- **Calf's brain** / Cervelle de veau — `cervelle-de-veau` · cuts · rare  
  Nothing else in the atlas behaves like it: soaked to bleed white, membranes lifted, poached in court-bouillon and then browned in noisette butter, ending as a set custard rather than as meat.
- **Calf's foot** / Pied de veau — `pied-de-veau` · cuts  
  Bought as a setting agent rather than as food: split into a daube or a tripe pot it supplies the gelatine that makes the sauce cling and the terrine set, which the fattier pig's trotter does less cleanly.
- **Calf's head** / Tête de veau — `tete-de-veau` · cuts  
  Bought boned and rolled by the metre, it is the only item that delivers gelatinous skin, cheek, palate and tongue-root as one cylinder, poached in a blanc and sliced — quite unlike the finished head cheese already listed.
- **Calf's liver** / Foie de veau — `foie-de-veau` · cuts · luxe  
  The atlas has cod liver and monkfish liver but no mammal liver at all; calf's liver is the pale, almost pink, mild one, cut in thick slices and cooked barely past rosy.
- **Calf's mesentery** / Fraise de veau — `fraise-de-veau` · cuts · rare  
  Not stomach and not intestine but the frilled membrane that carries them, named for the ruff it resembles; sold pre-cooked, it is the defining filling of true Lyonnaise andouillette and eats cold in vinaigrette.
- **Chicken cartilage (nankotsu)** / Cartilage de volaille (nankotsu) — `nankotsu` · cuts  
  The keel and knee cartilage sold by name for crunch alone; it carries no meat flavour and is grilled or fried until it shatters, which is the entire reason a kitchen buys it.
- **Chicken feet** / Pieds de volaille — `pied-de-volaille` · cuts  
  The cheapest and most concentrated gelatine source in a stockpot, and separately a dim-sum item braised and deep-fried until the skin blisters — a different purchase, and a different price, from carcass or wings.
- **Chicken gizzard** / Gésier de volaille — `gesier-de-volaille` · cuts  
  Raw gizzard is a hard-working muscle that stays crunchy when grilled fast as yakitori sunagimo — a completely different eating from the slow-confited duck gizzards already listed, which are soft and fat-cured.
- **Chicken liver** / Foie de volaille — `foie-de-volaille` · cuts  
  Small, soft and emulsifiable in a way no larger liver is, which is why it and not veal liver makes parfait and the Bressan gâteau de foies blonds; the pale corn-fed foies blonds are the version chefs order by name.
- **Chicken tail (bonjiri)** / Croupion de volaille (bonjiri) — `bonjiri` · cuts · rare  
  The nub above the vent, one per bird, carrying the preen gland and the fattiest flesh on the chicken — it self-bastes on the grill and tastes nothing like the lean thigh or the poultry oyster already listed.
- **Cockerel testicles** / Rognons de coq — `rognons-de-coq` · cuts · rare  
  Despite the name these are not kidneys but the cockerel's paired glands — pale, custardy, poached whole — and they are the second half of the classical garniture financière alongside cockscombs.
- **Cockscomb** / Crête de coq — `crete-de-coq` · cuts · rare  
  Pure collagen and skin rather than flesh: blanched, peeled of its outer membrane and simmered for hours to a translucent gelatinous strip, a texture no other item in the atlas provides.
- **Cod tongues** / Langues de morue — `langue-de-morue` · seafood · rare  
  The same anatomical piece as a kokotxa but from cod and almost always salt-cured, so it arrives stiff and must be desalted for a day before it can be floured and fried — a different product with different handling from fresh hake kokotxas.
- **Crab tomalley (kani miso)** / Crème de crabe (kani miso) — `tomalley` · seafood  
  The olive-green hepatopancreas, not the roe — the atlas lists lobster coral and scallop coral, which are gonads; tomalley is the liver, and it is emulsified into sauces or eaten straight from the shell.
- **Duck heart** / Cœur de canard — `coeur-de-canard` · cuts  
  A dense worked muscle, not an organ in texture — Gascon kitchens grill it rare on skewers or confit it in duck fat, and it eats closer to a tiny steak than to any liver or gland.
- **Duck tongue** / Langue de canard — `langue-de-canard` · cuts · rare  
  Barely five centimetres, built round a Y-shaped cartilage that stays crisp after braising, so it is eaten for that contrast of soft meat against crunch — a Cantonese and Sichuan banquet item with no French equivalent.
- **Fugu shirako** / Laitance de fugu — `fugu-shirako` · seafood · both  
  A different species from the cod milt that "shirako" normally means, and priced an order of magnitude higher: firmer, sweeter, served raw or blistered over charcoal rather than poached in broth.
- **Gras-double (rumen tripe)** / Gras-double — `gras-double` · cuts  
  The first of the four stomachs, sold blanched and flat: thick, smooth, ivory, and the only tripe firm enough to cut into the wide flaps that Lyon crumbs as tablier de sapeur or fries with onions.
- **Honeycomb tripe** / Bonnet — `bonnet` · cuts  
  The second stomach, prized above the other three for the pockets that hold sauce; it is the tripe specified for trippa alla romana and Spanish callos, and it stays tender where gras-double stays firm.
- **Kinkan (unlaid egg yolks)** / Kinkan (œufs non pondus) — `kinkan` · cuts · rare  
  The graded chain of yolks found inside a hen at slaughter, shell-less and increasing in size; richer and denser than any laid yolk, recovered only when whole birds are broken down, and skewered whole in high-end yakitori.
- **Kokotxas (hake throat)** / Kokotxas (gorges de merlu) — `kokotxas` · seafood · luxe  
  The V-shaped strip under the hake's chin, bought by name in Basque kitchens at many times the price of the fillet, and cooked at a temperature no fillet tolerates: barely simmered so its own gelatine emulsifies olive oil into pil-pil.
- **Konowata** / Konowata — `konowata` · seafood · both  
  The salted, fermented intestine of the sea cucumber, counted as one of Japan's three great delicacies; it is a briny liquid condiment-delicacy with nothing in common with the dried sea cucumber body already listed.
- **Kuchiko (dried sea cucumber ovaries)** / Kuchiko — `kuchiko` · seafood · both  
  A different organ again from konowata: the ovaries, salted and sun-dried into translucent amber triangles that are toasted before serving, and needing up to a hundred animals per piece.
- **Lamb brain** / Cervelle d'agneau — `cervelle-agneau` · cuts · rare  
  Roughly a fifth the size of a calf's brain, so it is cooked whole and whole-fried rather than sliced — the standard of the Spanish sesos and the Levantine repertoire, where the calf version is not used.
- **Lamb fries (testicles)** / Animelles — `animelles` · cuts · rare  
  A gland nothing else on the list covers: peeled of its tough tunic, soaked, sliced and fried, giving a soft creamy interior closer to brain than to muscle; Spain sells the bull version as criadillas.
- **Lamb kidney** / Rognon d'agneau — `rognon-agneau` · cuts  
  Sold by the piece, halved and skewered rather than carved, and cooked to pink in ninety seconds — a different handling and a different dish from the veal kidney's long low roast.
- **Lamb sweetbread** / Ris d'agneau — `ris-agneau` · cuts · rare  
  A different animal and a different order of size — walnut-sized, no pressing, no long degorging, straight into a hot pan for two minutes — where veal sweetbread needs soaking, blanching and weighting.
- **Lamb tongue** / Langue d'agneau — `langue-agneau` · cuts  
  Small enough to serve whole per portion after peeling, which neither ox nor veal tongue allows, and it is the tongue that goes warm into salads and into the Moroccan and Turkish repertoires.
- **Lamb tripe** / Panse d'agneau — `panse-agneau` · cuts · rare  
  Thinner and more pliable than beef tripe, which is why it can be cut into squares and tied into the parcels of pieds et paquets and of Auvergne tripoux — beef tripe is too rigid to fold.
- **Lampredotto (abomasum)** / Caillette (lampredotto) — `lampredotto` · cuts · rare  
  The fourth and true stomach, darker and more strongly flavoured than the three fore-stomachs; in France it mostly goes to rennet production, and Florence is effectively the one market that sells it as food.
- **Leaf lard** / Panne de porc — `panne-de-porc` · fats  
  The soft crumbly fat around the pig's kidneys, distinct from back fat and from rendered saindoux: it has almost no pork flavour and the highest melting point, which is why pastry cooks buy it specifically.
- **Mefun (salted salmon kidney)** / Mefun — `mefun` · seafood · rare  
  The blood-red strip running along the salmon's backbone, salted and matured until near-black; an Ainu-origin Hokkaido product with a mineral sweetness quite unlike any salmon roe or flesh on the list.
- **Monkfish cheek** / Joue de lotte — `joue-de-lotte` · seafood · luxe  
  A single round nugget of dense white muscle from each side of the head, sold separately from the tail at a premium, and cooked like a scallop — seared hard and briefly — where the tail is roasted on the bone.
- **Muzzle** / Museau de bœuf — `museau` · cuts  
  Sold already cooked and thinly sliced by the tripier, it is cartilage and skin with a crunch that no cheek or head cheese has, and it goes cold into vinaigrette rather than into a terrine.
- **Natural casings** / Boyaux naturels — `boyaux-naturels` · cuts  
  A charcutier's raw material bought by exact calibre and name — menu de mouton, menu de porc, chaudin, baudruche — where the choice sets the sausage's diameter, snap and drying rate; the atlas lists the finished sausages but not this.
- **Pig's bladder** / Vessie de porc — `vessie-de-porc` · cuts · rare  
  Bought as a cooking vessel, not as food: soaked, inflated and tied round a bird or a fish so it steams in its own vapour, the technique behind poularde en vessie and canard en vessie.
- **Pig's blood** / Sang de porc — `sang-de-porc` · cuts  
  Bought fresh and defibrinated as a liquid ingredient in its own right — it thickens a civet, sets a sanquette and binds boudin — where the atlas currently lists only the finished black pudding.
- **Pig's ear** / Oreille de porc — `oreille-de-porc` · cuts  
  Skin sandwiching a sheet of cartilage: braised until the collagen gives, then pressed and either crumbed and fried or sliced into salads, giving a gelatine-and-crunch texture no other cut on the list produces.
- **Pig's tail** / Queue de porc — `queue-de-porc` · cuts  
  Where oxtail is bought for the meat between the vertebrae, the pig's tail has almost none — it is braised for its skin and cartilage and then crisped, so it is a texture purchase, not a braising meat.
- **Pig's trotter** / Pied de porc — `pied-de-porc` · cuts  
  Mostly skin, tendon and small bones, so it is cooked for its gelatine and then either boned and stuffed or crumbed à la Sainte-Menehould — bought whole and by the pair, unlike any muscle cut.
- **Pork cheek** / Joue de porc — `joue-de-porc` · cuts  
  Beef cheek is a 400 g braise of four hours; pork cheek is an 80 g nugget that is tender in ninety minutes and carries far more intramuscular fat — and it is the raw muscle behind guanciale, which the atlas lists only in its cured state.
- **Pork liver** / Foie de porc — `foie-de-porc` · cuts  
  The terrine liver: too strong and grainy to serve in slices, but it is the binding, iron-heavy backbone of pâté de campagne, gâteau de foie and caillette, where calf's liver would be a waste and would not set.
- **Pork rind** / Couenne — `couenne` · fats  
  Skin with the fat scraped off, bought in sheets to line a cassoulet or daube so it releases gelatine and keeps the base from catching — a structural ingredient, unlike the rendered lard and back fat already listed.
- **Pork uterus (kobukuro)** / Utérus de porc (kobukuro) — `kobukuro` · cuts · rare  
  A lean, almost fat-free tube whose whole appeal is a squeaky snap under the tooth; it is grilled for seconds over charcoal in the horumon repertoire, and overcooking destroys it — nothing else behaves this way.
- **Poutine (Niçoise whitebait)** / Poutine — `poutine` · seafood · both  
  Sardine and anchovy fry taken under a 45-day derogation in four Riviera prud'homies only; transparent, eaten whole within hours, and legally a different catch from any adult fish on the list.
- **Shutō (fermented bonito viscera)** / Shutō — `shuto` · seafood · rare  
  Bonito stomach and intestine cured in salt for months into a dark, sharply savoury paste — a different animal and a different organ from the squid shiokara already listed, and used as a seasoning as much as a delicacy.
- **Skate liver** / Foie de raie — `foie-de-raie` · seafood · rare  
  Unlike cod liver, which is preserved and tinned, skate liver is bought fresh from the fishmonger on the day, is nearly liquid when warm, and is used as a rich sauce base or poached and served on toast.
- **Spleen** / Rate — `rate` · cuts · rare  
  Intensely mineral and blood-iron in a way no liver is, and it collapses to a paste rather than holding slices — the reason it appears only where that is the point: Tuscan crostini neri and Palermo's pani ca' meusa.
- **Udder** / Tétine — `tetine` · cuts · rare  
  Sold from the tripier already boiled, chalk-white and sliceable, with a firm waxy bite between fat and gland; it is grilled or crumbed in Lyon bouchons and has no equivalent texture anywhere else on the counter.
- **Veal heart** / Cœur de veau — `coeur-de-veau` · cuts  
  Lean to the point of having almost no fat, so it is either sliced across the grain and flashed like minute steak or larded and braised whole — the opposite treatments to any other veal offal.
- **Veal kidney** / Rognon de veau — `rognon-de-veau` · cuts · luxe  
  The generic "Kidneys" entry cannot cover this: veal kidney is a single multi-lobed mass sold still jacketed in its own fat, roasted whole and carved, and it is the mild one — nothing like the pungent single-lobe kidneys of older animals.
- **Veal sweetbread heart (noix)** / Ris de veau — noix (pomme) — `ris-de-veau-noix` · cuts · luxe  
  The existing generic "Sweetbreads / Ris de veau" hides the trade's only real split: the round thoracic lobe is bought as noix or pomme de ris at roughly double the price of the throat piece, and it is the only part firm enough to press, skin and roast whole.
- **Veal throat sweetbread (gorge)** / Ris de veau — gorge — `ris-de-veau-gorge` · cuts  
  The elongated cervical lobe is a separate SKU at a separate price, softer and more ragged than the noix, and it goes to braises, quenelle farces and vol-au-vent garnish rather than to a whole-roasted centrepiece.
- **Veal tongue** / Langue de veau — `langue-de-veau` · cuts  
  A quarter the weight of the ox tongue already listed, paler and far more delicate, poached in two hours rather than five and served hot with sauce ravigote instead of pressed and sliced cold.

- **Suan sun** / Pousses de bambou fermentées — `suan-sun` · vegetables · rare

- **Takana-zuke** / Takana-zuke — `takana-zuke` · condiments · rare

- **Throuba Thassos olive** / Olive throumba de Thassos — `olive-throuba` · condiments · rare

- **Zante currant** / Raisin de Corinthe — `raisin-de-corinthe` · nuts

- **Zha cai** / Zha cai — `zha-cai` · condiments  
  The swollen mustard stem, salted, pressed, chilli-rubbed and fermented into a hard, salty knob sliced for crunch — a stem pickle, not a leaf one.

### rare-fish — 46

- **Albacore** / Germon (thon blanc) — `albacore` · seafood  
  Pale, almost white flesh with a mild flavour, line-caught off the Basque coast for ttoro and for the best conserves — bought under its own name and never confused with bluefin.
- **Allis shad** / Alose — `allis-shad` · seafood · rare

- **Bonito (katsuo)** / Bonite à ventre rayé — `bonito` · seafood

- **Brown scorpionfish** / Rascasse brune — `brown-scorpionfish` · seafood · rare

- **Dentex** / Denté — `dentex` · seafood · luxe

- **Glass eel** / Civelle (pibale) — `glass-eel` · seafood · both

- **Grayling** / Ombre commun — `grayling` · seafood · rare  
  A cold-river salmonid whose fresh skin carries a thyme scent that fades within hours, so it is only worth cooking near the water; rarely sold commercially.
- **Greater amberjack (kanpachi)** / Sériole couronnée (kanpachi) — `kanpachi` · seafood · luxe  
  A separate Seriola species from buri: leaner, firmer, at its best in summer when buri is poor, and bought under its own name at a different price.
- **Grey mullet** / Mulet — `grey-mullet` · seafood  
  An estuary fish with grey, oily flesh that must be skinned and rested to lose muddiness; also the source of the roe used for poutargue, and cheap where its roe is not.
- **Grouper** / Mérou — `grouper` · seafood · luxe  
  Thick, gelatinous-skinned white flesh that holds together under long braising and yields a setting stock from the bones — a large-fish behaviour no bream or bass in the atlas offers.
- **Haddock** / Églefin — `haddock` · seafood  
  Finer-flaked and sweeter than cod, and the only white fish of the group traditionally cold-smoked, which is a different product again from the fresh fillet.
- **Halfbeak (sayori)** / Demi-bec du Japon (sayori) — `sayori` · seafood · rare  
  A needle-thin spring fish with a black-lined belly cavity that must be scraped clean; the flesh is clear and faintly sweet, and it is cut into ribbons or knots rather than slices.
- **Halibut** / Flétan — `halibut` · seafood · luxe  
  The largest flatfish, cut into thick loin steaks rather than filleted, with dense flaking white flesh that suits low-temperature roasting — nothing like sole or turbot in handling.
- **Japanese Spanish mackerel (sawara)** / Thazard oriental (sawara) — `sawara` · seafood · rare  
  A large pale-fleshed mackerel relative with soft, easily-broken flesh handled like a white fish — straw-seared tataki or saikyo-miso grilling — not like the small Atlantic mackerel.
- **Japanese amberjack (buri)** / Sériole du Japon (buri) — `buri` · seafood · luxe  
  Winter-fat yellowtail with pink, heavily marbled flesh used raw or in buri-daikon; the young form sold as hamachi is the same fish at a different age, not a separate entry.
- **Japanese red seabream (madai)** / Daurade japonaise (madai) — `madai` · seafood · luxe

- **Japanese tilefish (amadai)** / Tile du Japon (amadai) — `amadai` · seafood · luxe  
  Bought specifically for its scales: the Kyoto matsukasa-yaki technique fries the scaled skin upright into crisp pine-cone shingles over sweet, soft flesh — no other fish here is cooked scales-on.
- **Lamprey** / Lamproie — `lamprey` · seafood · rare  
  A jawless fish with no bones, bled live so its blood can thicken the red wine sauce it is cooked in — the Bordeaux lamproie à la bordelaise, a technique unique in the repertoire.
- **Largehead hairtail (tachiuo)** / Poisson-sabre (tachiuo) — `tachiuo` · seafood · rare  
  A ribbon-shaped fish with edible silver guanine skin and no scales, grilled or seared so the skin blisters; soft, delicate flesh that falls apart if handled like a round fish.
- **Lemon sole** / Limande-sole — `lemon-sole` · seafood  
  A softer, sweeter, thinner flatfish than Dover sole that cannot take the same firm meunière treatment; sold and priced as a separate fish in every French poissonnerie.
- **Meagre** / Maigre (ombrine) — `meagre` · seafood · rare  
  A large drum with dense, low-fat flesh that stays firm and slices into thick steaks; the wild estuary fish is a spring event on the French Atlantic coast and priced apart from farmed stock.
- **Monkfish liver (ankimo)** / Foie de lotte (ankimo) — `ankimo` · seafood · luxe  
  The liver, deveined, sake-soaked and steamed into a rolled terrine — the sea's foie gras, an offal with its own season and technique, unrelated to the monkfish tail already listed.
- **Olive flounder (hirame)** / Cardeau hirame — `hirame` · seafood · luxe  
  A left-eyed flounder bought for raw work: firm, near-translucent flesh sliced usuzukuri thin, plus the separately-priced engawa fin muscle — a different animal and different use from turbot, brill or Dover sole.
- **Pacific saury (sanma)** / Balaou du Japon (sanma) — `sanma` · seafood  
  A slim, intensely oily autumn fish grilled whole and eaten with its bitter guts intact — a deliberate flavour component, unlike any other small blue fish in the atlas.
- **Perch** / Perche — `perch` · seafood  
  Small firm white lake fillets, sweet and entirely bone-free once trimmed, fried in butter — a different flesh and a different market from pike or zander.
- **Pike conger (hamo)** / Congre-brochet (hamo) — `hamo` · seafood · rare  
  Riddled with fine intramuscular bones, so it is bought only by kitchens able to do honekiri — scoring the flesh every millimetre without cutting the skin — then blanched to bloom.
- **Pollack** / Lieu jaune — `pollack` · seafood  
  The prized golden cousin of coley: large translucent flakes and a firmness that takes searing, bought line-caught by Breton chefs at several times the price of lieu noir.
- **Red porgy** / Pagre — `red-porgy` · seafood · rare  
  A separate sparid genus from the gilt-head bream: rosier, coarser-grained flesh that suits roasting whole rather than the fine raw slicing bream is used for.
- **Red scorpionfish (chapon)** / Chapon (rascasse rouge) — `red-scorpionfish` · seafood · luxe  
  The deep-water large scorpionfish, bought whole at 1–3 kg to be roasted or steamed as a centrepiece — a completely different purchase from the small inshore soup rascasse.
- **Saltwater conger (anago)** / Congre japonais (anago) — `anago` · seafood · rare  
  A small marine conger simmered soft in sweet nikiri and glazed, giving a fragile melting texture that is the opposite of the firm grilled freshwater eel already listed.
- **Sea trout** / Truite de mer — `sea-trout` · seafood · rare  
  The sea-run form of brown trout: pale coral flesh, firmer and less fatty than farmed trout or salmon, wild-caught in a short season and sold at its own price.
- **Smelt** / Éperlan — `smelt` · seafood · rare  
  A small estuary fish that smells distinctly of cucumber when truly fresh, eaten whole — head, bones and all — after a flour dusting and a hard fry.
- **Splendid alfonsino (kinmedai)** / Béryx long (kinmedai) — `kinmedai` · seafood · both  
  A deep-water red fish with soft, oily white flesh that is cooked skin-on with the scarlet skin left intact — nishime simmering or skin-blanched sashimi — behaving nothing like any bream or snapper already listed.
- **Striped jack (shima-aji)** / Carangue dentue (shima-aji) — `shima-aji` · seafood · luxe  
  A different genus from the horse mackerels, with clean firm white flesh and no oily blue-fish character — priced and ordered separately from aji at every Japanese counter.
- **Sturgeon** / Esturgeon — `sturgeon` · seafood · luxe  
  The flesh, not the roe: cartilaginous-skeletoned, firm and almost meaty, it is smoked or roasted in thick pieces and behaves more like veal than like fish.
- **Sweetfish (ayu)** / Ayu — `ayu` · seafood · rare  
  A river fish whose flesh and guts smell of melon and cucumber from its algae diet; grilled whole on a skewer with the bitter viscera left in, and worthless once out of season.
- **Swordfish** / Espadon — `swordfish` · seafood  
  Sold as boneless steaks of dense, low-moisture flesh that grills like meat and dries out past medium — a texture no other fish in the atlas shares.
- **Thornyhead (kinki)** / Kinki (sébaste épineux) — `kinki` · seafood · luxe  
  A deep-cold-water rockfish with fat laid between the muscle fibres so it stays moist under long nitsuke simmering; one of the most expensive fish per kilo in Hokkaido.
- **Tiger pufferfish (torafugu)** / Fugu (poisson-globe) — `fugu` · seafood · both  
  Legally restricted to licensed handlers because of tetrodotoxin in the organs; bought for gelatinous, near-flavourless flesh sliced transparently thin and for the prized shirako and skin.
- **Tuna belly (toro)** / Ventrèche de thon rouge (toro) — `tuna-belly-toro` · cuts · luxe  
  The fatty ventral cut is bought, priced and cut as its own product — soft, marbled, served raw and never cooked like the lean loin the atlas already lists as tuna.
- **Vendace roe (Kalix löjrom)** / Œufs de corégone — `vendace-roe` · seafood · luxe

- **Weever** / Vive — `weever` · seafood · rare  
  Venomous dorsal spines must be cut off at the quayside before it can be handled; the reward is firm, sweet flesh used in bouillabaisse and, filleted, grilled on its own.
- **Whitefish (féra)** / Féra (corégone) — `whitefish-fera` · seafood · rare

- **Whiting** / Merlan — `whiting` · seafood  
  Extremely fine, fragile flesh that collapses unless crumbed and fried — the merlan Colbert and the base of classic fish mousselines and quenelles.

- **Nashi pear** / Poire nashi — `nashi` · fruits  
  Apple-shaped, crunchy and granular with high water content, it stays crisp when cut and is used raw in salads and pickles rather than cooked like European pears.

- **Seville orange** / Bigarade — `bigarade` · fruits · rare

- **Shikuwasa** / Shikuwasa — `shikuwasa` · fruits · both

- **Sloe** / Prunelle — `prunelle` · fruits · rare  
  A wild, astringent, tannic hedgerow fruit picked after frost and used only infused, in liqueur or as a sharp condiment paste — never as a plum.
- **Sour cherry** / Griotte — `griotte` · fruits · rare  
  A different species from the sweet cherry: too acid to eat raw, it is the cherry of clafoutis, kirsch, and sauces for duck and game.
- **Soursop** / Corossol — `corossol` · fruits · rare  
  Fibrous and markedly acidic where the cherimoya is sweet and smooth, so it is sieved to juice or sorbet rather than eaten in the hand.
- **Star fruit** / Carambole — `carambole` · fruits  
  Crisp, waxy and oxalic-sour, sliced across for its star section and used raw or lightly pickled; the acidity comes from oxalic rather than citric acid.
- **Sudachi** / Sudachi — `sudachi` · fruits · both  
  A Japanese acid citrus used only unripe and green for its zest and sharp, pine-like juice over grilled fish — no relation in flavour or handling to yuzu, which is used ripe and yellow for its perfumed peel.
- **Tamarillo** / Tamarillo — `tamarillo` · fruits · rare  
  A botanical fruit that behaves savoury — tomato acidity with passion-fruit seeds and a bitter skin that must be peeled — used in chutneys and with game as often as in dessert.
- **Tamarind** / Tamarin — `tamarin` · fruits  
  Bought as a sour pod pulp and used as a base acid in sauces and braises, filling a role no vinegar or citrus in the list covers.
- **Vineyard peach** / Pêche de vigne — `peche-de-vigne` · fruits · rare  
  Small, blood-red fleshed, very late and intensely perfumed with low sugar; it is poached in wine and made into sorbet, and its short season does not overlap the ordinary peach's.
- **Wild bilberry** / Myrtille sauvage — `myrtille-sauvage` · fruits · both  
  A different species from the cultivated blueberry: purple-fleshed all through, so it stains and colours a preparation, with far more acidity and a fraction of the size.
- **Wild strawberry** / Fraise des bois — `fraise-des-bois` · fruits · both  
  A different species, thumbnail-sized and intensely perfumed, sold by the punnet at a multiple of the garden strawberry's price and used only scattered raw.
- **Williams pear** / Poire Williams — `poire-williams` · fruits  
  The musky, high-aroma pear that holds up to poaching and is the only pear used for eau-de-vie and sorbet; it is bought by name, not as generic pear.

### shellfish — 41

- **American lobster** / Homard américain (canadien) — `american-lobster` · seafood  
  A different species from the Breton homard, with softer, wetter flesh that will not take the same roasting, and a shell-hardness cycle that decides whether it is worth cooking at all.
- **Ark shell** / Akagai (arche) — `akagai` · seafood · luxe

- **Belon flat oyster** / Huître plate (belon) — `belon-flat-oyster` · seafood · both

- **Brown shrimp** / Crevette grise — `brown-shrimp` · seafood

- **Burgundy snail** / Escargot de Bourgogne — `escargot-de-bourgogne` · seafood · rare

- **Carabinero prawn** / Gambon écarlate (carabinero) — `carabinero` · seafood · luxe

- **Common prawn** / Bouquet (crevette rose) — `common-prawn` · seafood

- **Dog cockle** / Amande de mer — `dog-cockle` · seafood · rare

- **Dungeness crab** / Crabe dormeur du Pacifique — `dungeness-crab` · seafood  
  Nuttier and sweeter than the tourteau, with a high meat-to-shell yield and a tradition of being sold whole and already cooked, which changes how a kitchen buys and stores it.
- **Geoduck** / Panope (geoduck) — `geoduck` · seafood · both  
  Two ingredients in one animal: the siphon is blanched, peeled and sliced translucent for raw service, while the mantle is a different, softer meat that must be cooked.
- **Hairy crab** / Crabe poilu de Shanghai — `mitten-crab` · seafood · both  
  Bought for six autumn weeks for its roe alone — the body meat is incidental — steamed whole with perilla and eaten with ginger vinegar, an entirely seasonal ritual product.
- **Hotate scallop** / Hotate (Saint-Jacques du Japon) — `hotate` · seafood · luxe  
  Larger and denser-grained than Pecten maximus, with enough structure to be sliced raw for sashimi, which is why the Japanese name travels with the product.
- **Japanese abalone** / Awabi (ormeau du Japon) — `awabi` · seafood · both  
  Several times the size of the Breton ormeau and handled the opposite way: sliced raw for sashimi or steamed for hours in sake and dashi, rather than beaten and pan-fried in butter.
- **Kumamoto oyster** / Huître kumamoto — `kumamoto-oyster` · seafood · luxe  
  Its own species, thumb-sized with a very deep cup and a melon-sweet finish that carries almost no salt, which is why raw bars sell it as the beginner's oyster and the connoisseur's last one.
- **Kuruma prawn** / Crevette kuruma — `kuruma-ebi` · seafood · luxe  
  Sold and shipped alive in sawdust, banded brown and blue, and cooked seconds after killing for tempura or nigiri — its sweetness is a function of that liveness.
- **Mantis shrimp** / Squille (crevette-mante) — `mantis-shrimp` · seafood · rare  
  Neither shrimp nor lobster but a stomatopod, with a flat translucent tail freed by running scissors down both sides — sweeter than langoustine and only worth eating within a day of landing.
- **Murex** / Murex (rocher) — `murex` · seafood · rare  
  A spiny predatory snail from the Mediterranean rocks with a frankly bitter, deeply iodised flesh — boiled and picked, it is the strongest-tasting shellfish on a Marseille plateau.
- **Nantucket bay scallop** / Pétoncle de baie — `bay-scallop` · seafood · both  
  A wild fishery of five months, hand-shucked into nuggets the size of a fingernail with an intense sugar that collapses under real heat — served raw or barely warmed through.
- **Northern sweet shrimp** / Crevette nordique (amaebi) — `amaebi` · seafood  
  The one shrimp eaten raw as standard practice, its flesh almost gelatinous with sweetness, while the head is fried separately as a second component.
- **Olympia oyster** / Huître olympia — `olympia-oyster` · seafood · rare  
  The only oyster native to the American Pacific coast, coin-sized after four years and coppery-celery in flavour, harvested in numbers too small to leave the West Coast.
- **Pen shell** / Tairagai (jambonneau) — `tairagai` · seafood · luxe  
  A single large adductor muscle from a fan-shaped shell buried upright in the sand, grainier and more mineral than a scallop, seared on one face or served raw.
- **Periwinkle** / Bigorneau — `periwinkle` · seafood  
  Pea-sized and picked out with a pin, boiled briefly and eaten cold and salty as a plateau counterpoint — it is a texture and a ritual more than a portion of meat.
- **Petit-gris snail** / Petit-gris — `petit-gris` · seafood  
  Half the size of the Bourgogne and markedly more tender and less earthy, which is why Provençal and modern kitchens use it in broths and ragoûts where the big snail would dominate.
- **Quahog** / Palourde américaine (quahog) — `quahog` · seafood  
  A much harder, thicker shell than the palourde and meat that toughens fast, so the trade sizes it into littleneck for raw service and chowder-grade for long simmering.
- **Queen conch** / Lambi — `conch` · seafood  
  A muscle so tough it is pounded flat or pressure-cooked before anything else can be done with it, and the base of French Antillean fricassée and colombo.
- **Queen scallop** / Pétoncle — `queen-scallop` · seafood  
  A third the size of a Saint-Jacques, sweeter and sold already shucked, cooking in seconds — treat it as a Saint-Jacques and it is ruined.
- **Red prawn of Palamós** / Gamba rouge de Palamós — `gamba-rouge` · seafood · luxe  
  A different deep-sea species from the carabinero, smaller and finer, sweet enough to be served raw or seared for twenty seconds — heat past that turns it to cotton.
- **Slipper lobster** / Cigale de mer — `slipper-lobster` · seafood · both  
  Flat, clawless and antenna-less, with a short broad tail sweeter than langouste; protected in French waters since 1992, so kitchens source it from Italian and Spanish landings.
- **Snow crab** / Crabe des neiges — `snow-crab` · seafood · luxe  
  Long thin legs yielding fine sweet strands rather than the dense blocks of king crab, sold as cooked clusters and served cold far more often than hot.
- **Soft-shell clam** / Mye — `soft-shell-clam` · seafood  
  A brittle shell that never fully closes around a protruding siphon, so it must be purged in salt water for hours and the siphon skin peeled after steaming.
- **Soft-shell crab** / Crabe mou — `soft-shell-crab` · seafood  
  A blue crab caught in the hours after moulting and eaten entire, shell included — a separate product graded by moult size, not a way of serving hard crab.
- **Spiny lobster** / Langouste rouge — `spiny-lobster` · seafood · luxe  
  No claws at all, so the whole animal is a tail — firmer, drier and sweeter than homard, butchered along the back rather than split, and it gives no claw meat to a dish.
- **Stone crab claw** / Pince de crabe de pierre — `stone-crab-claw` · seafood · luxe  
  Only the claw is landed — the crab is returned alive to regrow it — and it is always cooked at sea and sold chilled, so it never reaches a kitchen raw.
- **Surf clam** / Hokkigai (mactre) — `hokkigai` · seafood  
  Its foot turns scarlet the instant it is blanched — a few seconds of heat, never more — producing a crunchy sweet slice unlike any raw clam.
- **Sydney rock oyster** / Huître de roche de Sydney — `sydney-rock-oyster` · seafood · rare  
  A separate genus that grows for three years instead of one, giving a dense, long mineral finish that Australian kitchens rank above any imported gigas.
- **Velvet swimming crab** / Étrille — `velvet-crab` · seafood  
  Too small to pick for meat, it is bought for its shell: the crab that gives soupe de poissons and bisque their depth, crushed whole rather than served.
- **Violet sea squirt** / Violet (figue de mer) — `violet-sea-squirt` · seafood · rare

- **Warty venus clam** / Praire — `praire` · seafood

- **Wedge clam** / Telline — `telline` · seafood · rare

- **Whelk** / Bulot — `whelk` · seafood  
  A large sea snail that must be simmered in an aromatic court-bouillon for twenty minutes and cooled in it, or it turns to rubber — a completely different treatment from any bivalve.

  Tightly rolled pellets that unfurl slowly and take heavy sugar and mint without collapsing — the structural reason it, and not any other green, is the base of Maghrebi mint tea and its granités.
- **Gyokuro** / Gyokuro — `gyokuro` · condiments · luxe  
  Shaded for three weeks before picking, which suppresses catechins and builds theanine, so it brews at 50–60 °C into a thick umami liquor used as a sauce base or dashi substitute — a different product from unshaded sencha and from powdered matcha.
- **Honeybush** / Honeybush — `honeybush` · herbs · rare  
  A different Cape shrub from rooibos, with an apricot-and-honey sweetness and no earthiness, used where rooibos would read as woody.
- **Jasmine pearl tea** / Perles de jasmin — `jasmine-pearl-tea` · condiments · luxe  
  Green tea buds hand-rolled into pearls and scented over several nights with living jasmine blossom — the aroma is absorbed floral, not added oil, and it survives infusion into cream where jasmine extract does not.
- **Keemun** / Keemun (Qimen) — `keemun` · condiments

- **Kombucha** / Kombucha — `kombucha` · condiments  
  Fermented sweetened tea used in kitchens as a low-acetic acidulant and glazing liquid — bought for its acidity and residual tea tannin, so it sits beside vinegar rather than beside tea.
- **Kukicha** / Kukicha — `kukicha` · condiments  
  Made from stems and leaf ribs rather than leaf blade, giving a creamy, nutty, near-caffeine-free infusion that carries dairy — a different raw material from any leaf tea, not a grade of one.
- **Labrador tea** / Thé du Labrador — `labrador-tea` · herbs · rare  
  Boreal shrub leaf with a resinous mint-and-fir aroma, infused into beurre blanc, game sauces and creams in Quebec kitchens; it is a wild aromatic, not a Camellia tea, and is used sparingly because of its ledol content.
- **Lapsang souchong** / Lapsang souchong (thé fumé) — `lapsang-souchong` · condiments  
  Black tea dried over smouldering pinewood, so it is bought as a smoking agent — laid in the tray under fish or scattered in a smoker — rather than as a beverage tea.
- **Longjing tea** / Thé Long Jing (Puits du Dragon) — `longjing-tea` · condiments · luxe  
  Pan-fired flat green leaf with a chestnut-and-butter note; it is cooked with, not merely infused — the leaves are stir-fried whole into Longjing shrimp — which no steamed Japanese green will do.
- **Oriental Beauty oolong** / Oolong Oriental Beauty (Bai Hao) — `oriental-beauty-oolong` · condiments · both  
  Heavily oxidised Taiwanese oolong whose honey-muscat aroma comes from leafhopper bites on the growing leaf, not from processing — a flavour no other tea can be made to produce.
- **Peppermint** / Menthe poivrée — `peppermint` · herbs  
  A sterile hybrid high in menthol, cold and pungent on the palate, unlike the sweet carvone-led spearmint sold as plain mint; the two are not interchangeable in an infusion or a chocolate.
- **Roasted barley tea** / Thé d'orge grillé (mugicha) — `mugicha` · grains  
  Whole barley roasted dark and infused: caffeine-free, bitter-toasted and bready, used as a cooking liquid and cold broth base — a grain infusion, with none of the tannin of leaf tea.
- **Roasted chicory root** / Chicorée torréfiée — `roasted-chicory-root` · condiments  
  Chicory taproot dried and roasted to a caffeine-free, bitter-caramel infusion with a syrupy body; a root, not a bean, and used both alone and cut into coffee in the north of France and in Louisiana.
- **Rooibos** / Rooibos — `rooibos` · herbs  
  Fermented South African legume shrub, tannin-free so it never turns bitter however long it steeps — the reason it is used for long infusions of milk, custard and brines.
- **Sencha** / Sencha — `sencha` · condiments  
  Unshaded, steamed then rolled leaf: grassy, astringent, brewed hot and short — the everyday Japanese green whose bitterness is the point, unlike shaded gyokuro or roasted hojicha.
- **Sheng pu-erh** / Pu-erh cru (sheng) — `sheng-puerh` · condiments · luxe  
  Sun-dried compressed leaf left to age slowly without wet-piling: bright, bitter, apricot-and-camphor, and still astringent enough to cut fat — nothing like the ripe version despite the shared name.
- **Shou pu-erh** / Pu-erh cuit (shou) — `shou-puerh` · condiments  
  Wet-piled in a controlled microbial fermentation, giving earthy, damp-wood, beetroot depth used to braise pork belly and duck — a different process and a different flavour from raw pu-erh, not an older one.
- **Silver Needle white tea** / Thé blanc Aiguilles d'argent (Yin Zhen) — `silver-needle-white-tea` · condiments · luxe  
  Unrolled, unfired downy buds only, withered and dried: a melon-and-hay infusion so delicate it is used cold-infused for shellfish and clear jellies, where any processed tea would dominate.
- **Tie Guan Yin oolong** / Oolong Tie Guan Yin — `tie-guan-yin` · condiments · luxe  
  Lightly oxidised, lightly roasted ball-rolled oolong with an orchid-and-cream aroma — the floral end of the oolong range, used to infuse creams and butters where a roasted oolong would read as smoke.
- **Yerba maté** / Maté (yerba maté) — `yerba-mate` · herbs  
  Dried holly leaf, often smoke-cured over wood, giving a green-tobacco bitterness that chefs use as a smoked infusion for game and for ice cream — a distinct plant family from Camellia tea.

### truffle2 — 25

- **Amethyst deceiver** / Laccaire améthyste — `amethyst-deceiver` · mushrooms · rare

- **Bamboo fungus** / Satyre voilé (champignon bambou) — `bamboo-fungus` · mushrooms · rare

- **Blonde morel** / Morille blonde — `blonde-morel` · mushrooms · luxe

- **Bloody milk cap** / Lactaire sanguin — `bloody-milk-cap` · mushrooms · rare

- **Charcoal burner** / Russule charbonnière — `charcoal-burner-russula` · mushrooms

- **Cordyceps flower** / Fleur de cordyceps — `cordyceps-flower` · mushrooms · rare  
  Cultivated orange fruiting spikes added whole to Cantonese double-boiled soups for a saffron colour and mild sweetness; used by the handful, not the gram.
- **Golden oyster** / Pleurote jaune — `golden-oyster` · mushrooms

- **Green cracking russula** / Russule verdoyante (palomet) — `green-cracking-russula` · mushrooms · rare  
  A crumbly-fleshed russula with a nutty, almost hazelnut taste eaten raw or grilled, unrelated in handling to any gilled forest mushroom already listed.
- **Hon-shimeji** / Hon-shimeji — `hon-shimeji` · mushrooms · luxe  
  The mycorrhizal species Japan legally protects from being labelled shimeji: firm, sweet, and priced near matsutake, not the cheap cultivated buna-shimeji.
- **Honey fungus (chiodini)** / Armillaire couleur de miel — `honey-fungus` · mushrooms  
  Must be blanched and the water discarded before use, then goes into Italian braises and preserves — a handling rule no other mushroom here shares.
- **Lobster mushroom** / Champignon homard — `lobster-mushroom` · mushrooms · rare  
  A parasitised russula whose orange crust gives a shellfish aroma and a dense, snappy texture that holds up to braising unlike any true gilled mushroom.
- **Orange birch bolete** / Bolet roux — `orange-birch-bolete` · mushrooms  
  A birch- and aspen-associated bolete whose flesh blackens on cooking, so it is treated as a stew and pickling mushroom rather than a cep substitute.
- **Oregon black truffle** / Truffe noire d'Oregon — `oregon-black-truffle` · mushrooms · luxe  
  Not a Tuber at all: its aroma is tropical fruit — pineapple, green apple, cherry — so it is used on desserts and dairy where Périgord truffle would be wrong.
- **Oregon white truffle** / Truffe blanche d'Oregon — `oregon-white-truffle` · mushrooms · luxe  
  A North American Tuber with a garlic-herb-and-hazelnut aroma quite unlike Alba white truffle, harvested from Douglas fir plantations and shaved raw within days of lifting.
- **Parasol mushroom** / Coulemelle — `parasol-mushroom` · mushrooms  
  Its wide flat cap is cooked like a schnitzel — breaded and pan-fried whole — a use no other wild mushroom on the list supports.
- **Pecan truffle** / Truffe de pécan — `pecan-truffle` · mushrooms · rare  
  Lifted from pecan orchards in the American South and bought by name for a smoky hickory-nut character no European truffle has.
- **Pink oyster** / Pleurote rose — `pink-oyster` · mushrooms  
  A tropical Pleurotus that fries to a chewy, bacon-like strip and loses its pink on heat; grown warm, so its supply season differs from grey oyster.
- **Straw mushroom** / Champignon de paille — `straw-mushroom` · mushrooms  
  Harvested inside its egg-shaped veil, giving a slippery double-layered bite; usable only fresh in Asia or canned elsewhere, never dried.
- **Summer cep** / Cèpe d'été — `summer-cep` · mushrooms · both  
  A separate Boletus that fruits weeks before the autumn cep, with a suede-dry finely cracked cap and softer flesh best used raw or barely cooked.
- **Termite mushroom (jizong)** / Champignon des termitières (jizong) — `termite-mushroom` · mushrooms · luxe  
  Grown by termites and impossible to farm, this Yunnan mushroom has a sweet, shredding, chicken-like flesh and the highest price of the Chinese wild season.

### truffles-fungi — 31

- **Bagnoli truffle** / Truffe de Bagnoli — `bagnoli-truffle` · mushrooms · rare  
  Known in France as the Lorraine or Meuse truffle, it carries a bituminous, phenolic aroma and a bitter-almond finish found in no other truffle; it is dosed in grams into terrines and sauces rather than shaved, and used to be refused outright by chefs.
- **Bianchetto truffle** / Truffe blanchette — `bianchetto-truffle` · mushrooms · both  
  The only white truffle of late winter and spring, filling the months when magnatum is gone: smaller, garlic-and-gas aroma that fades within days, so it is shaved at the very last second onto warm butter or eggs.
- **Black morel** / Morille conique — `black-morel` · mushrooms · both  
  Sold and priced apart from the morille blonde already listed: a narrower, darker, thicker-walled cap with a smokier and more assertive flavour that holds up in a cream sauce and dries better, where the blonde is sweeter and more fragile.
- **Blue foot mushroom** / Pied-bleu — `blue-foot-mushroom` · mushrooms  
  A violet-stemmed late-autumn mushroom with a distinctly perfumed, almost floral flavour; it must be cooked through and releases a lot of water, so it is dry-sautéed hard before any butter goes in.
- **Bronze bolete** / Cèpe bronzé — `bronze-bolete` · mushrooms · rare  
  Bought by name apart from the cèpe de Bordeaux and priced above it: a near-black cap under oak and chestnut in warm southern woods, arriving earlier in summer, with denser flesh that stays firm when sliced raw or roasted whole.
- **Burgundy truffle** / Truffe de Bourgogne — `burgundy-truffle` · mushrooms · both  
  Traded as its own product with its own season and price from the summer truffle already listed: harvested in autumn and winter with a dark, marbled gleba and a hazelnut-cocoa perfume strong enough to survive brief cooking, where aestivum is a mild, pale, high-summer truffle used raw.
- **Caesar's mushroom** / Oronge — `caesars-mushroom` · mushrooms · both  
  One of the very few forest mushrooms served raw: an orange-capped amanita of hot chestnut and oak woods, sliced thin with olive oil and parmesan for its sweet chestnut flavour, and cooking it is generally counted as wasting it.
- **Candy cap** / Candy cap — `candy-cap` · mushrooms · rare  
  The one mushroom used in pastry: dried, it develops a maple-and-burnt-sugar aroma that infuses into cream and ice cream, so it is bought dried by the gram and never treated as a savoury sauté.
- **Cauliflower mushroom** / Clavaire crépue — `cauliflower-mushroom` · mushrooms · rare  
  A mass of brittle noodle-like ribbons at the foot of pines, cleaned lobe by lobe because it traps pine litter; the texture stays crisp and springy after long cooking, unlike any cap-and-stem mushroom.
- **Chicken of the woods** / Polypore soufré — `chicken-of-the-woods` · mushrooms · rare  
  A sulphur-yellow bracket growing on living trunks, with a fibrous, layered flesh that pulls apart like poached chicken breast; it is treated as a protein substitute, must be taken young, and is inedible once it turns woody.
- **Chinese truffle** / Truffe de Chine — `chinese-truffle` · mushrooms  
  A separate Asian species that looks almost exactly like melanosporum but carries very little aroma and holds its firm texture through cooking; it is bought knowingly for colour, garnish and volume, and is the species behind most truffle adulteration cases.
- **Desert truffle** / Terfèze — `desert-truffle` · mushrooms · rare  
  Not a Tuber at all but a Saharan fungus symbiotic with helianthemum scrub: sandy, potato-firm, almost odourless, so it is peeled and braised in tagine or ravioli filling for texture rather than perfume.
- **Fairy ring mushroom** / Faux mousseron — `fairy-ring-mushroom` · mushrooms · rare  
  The tiny meadow mushroom that dries to an intense almond-and-hay powder — bought mostly dried, by the handful, as a seasoning for sauces and blanquettes, and not to be confused with the spring mousseron de la Saint-Georges, which is a large fresh mushroom.
- **Field mushroom** / Rosé des prés — `field-mushroom` · mushrooms  
  A different Agaricus from the cultivated button, gathered in grazed pasture: pink gills turning chocolate, far more aniseed-and-earth in flavour, and it blackens the pan and any cream it touches, which the cultivated one does not.
- **Giant puffball** / Vesse-de-loup géante — `giant-puffball` · mushrooms · rare  
  A football-sized ball of uniform white flesh with no gills or stem, so it is cut into steaks and pan-fried or breaded — a butchery-style use no other fungus allows; it is only edible while the interior is pure white.
- **Huitlacoche** / Huitlacoche — `huitlacoche` · mushrooms · rare  
  A smut fungus that swells the kernels of living corn into grey-black galls, harvested from the cob; it tastes of sweet corn crossed with earth and ink, blackens whatever it is cooked with, and has no relation to any forest mushroom.
- **Koji rice** / Riz koji — `koji-rice` · condiments  
  Steamed rice grown through with a cultivated mould, bought as a live enzymatic ingredient rather than as something to eat: it converts starch to sugar and protein to glutamate, and is the working agent behind miso and amazake, which are finished products already listed.
- **Lion's mane** / Hydne hérisson — `lions-mane` · mushrooms  
  A white cascade of soft spines with no cap at all; its flesh shreds into strands and browns like crab or scallop meat, which is exactly how kitchens use it, and it holds water so it is pressed and seared hard.
- **March hygrophorus** / Hygrophore de mars — `march-hygrophorus` · mushrooms · rare  
  Pushes up through melting snow under fir and beech, making it one of the only fresh wild mushrooms available in late winter alongside the bianchetto; grey, thick-fleshed and sweet, with a faint scent of faded rose.
- **Musky truffle** / Truffe musquée — `brumale-truffle` · mushrooms · rare  
  A different species that shares the black truffle's winter season but not its behaviour: coarse white veins, a penetrating musk-and-nutmeg aroma that turns aggressive when heated, so it is used in small quantity in cooked farces rather than shaved raw.
- **Nameko** / Nameko — `nameko` · mushrooms  
  Coated in a natural gelatinous film that dissolves into the cooking liquid and thickens it — the reason it is bought for miso soup and nabe, and the reason it must not be washed.
- **Pine bolete** / Cèpe des pins — `pine-bolete` · mushrooms · rare  
  The bolete of conifer and mountain ground, with a mahogany cap and a resinous, more acid flavour than the edulis of oak woods; it fruits in two short flushes, spring and late autumn, which is why the trade names it separately.
- **Pioppino** / Pholiote du peuplier — `pioppino` · mushrooms  
  Grown on poplar since Roman times: a slim brown cap on a long crisp stem with a marked hazelnut flavour, and almost uniquely among cultivated mushrooms it barely shrinks in the pan, so the stems are used as much as the caps.
- **Shaggy ink cap** / Coprin chevelu — `shaggy-ink-cap` · mushrooms · rare  
  Deliquesces into black ink within hours of picking, so it is cooked the day it is gathered and never appears in normal distribution; the flesh is delicate and almost milky, closer to a vegetable than to a forest mushroom.
- **Shimeji** / Shimeji — `shimeji` · mushrooms  
  Sold in dense clumps of small marbled caps that stay crunchy through cooking; raw it is unpleasantly bitter and needs real heat to sweeten, unlike the enoki it is often shelved beside.
- **Smooth black truffle** / Truffe noire lisse — `smooth-black-truffle` · mushrooms · both  
  A black-skinned truffle that smells like a white one — a pronounced garlic note, hence its trade nickname garlic truffle — so it is used raw like magnatum despite its melanosporum-like appearance, and it sits in the early autumn gap between the two.
- **Snow fungus** / Trémelle blanche — `snow-fungus` · mushrooms  
  A translucent frilled jelly bought dried and rehydrated to eight times its size; it is essentially flavourless and used for its crunch-in-jelly texture in sweet syrups and desserts, not in savoury cooking.
- **Sooty head** / Petit-gris — `sooty-head` · mushrooms · rare  
  A market mushroom of the Southwest and the pine hills, picked after the first frosts when the boletes have stopped: streaky grey cap, firm yellow-tinged flesh and a mealy-sweet flavour that suits a persillade rather than a cream sauce.
- **Truffle juice** / Jus de truffe — `truffle-juice` · condiments · luxe  
  The dark cooking liquor drawn off when truffles are sterilised in the tin — a separate pantry item with its own price, used by the spoonful to finish sauce Périgueux, risotto and vinaigrette, and giving depth no fresh truffle survives being cooked into.
- **Truffle oil** / Huile de truffe — `truffle-oil` · fats  
  A distinct product with almost no relation to the fungus: nearly all commercial bottles are neutral oil scented with synthesised 2,4-dithiapentane, which gives one loud persistent note that does not fade or cook off, unlike real truffle.
- **Winter chanterelle** / Chanterelle en tube — `winter-chanterelle` · mushrooms · rare  
  Runs from autumn into frost, months after the girolle is finished: a hollow-stemmed, brown-grey, papery-thin mushroom with a peppery finish that collapses in seconds in the pan, so it goes in at the very end rather than being seared.

- **Rat-tail radish pod** / Radis serpent — `rat-tail-radish` · vegetables · rare  
  Grown for its long seed pods, not its root — crisp, peppery, eaten raw or flash-pickled; a different edible organ from every radish already listed.
- **Red amaranth leaf** / Amarante rouge — `red-amaranth-leaf` · vegetables · rare  
  A leaf crop, not the grain: it bleeds magenta into the pan and tastes of earthy spinach, and is bought for that colour transfer.
- **Rocambole garlic** / Ail rocambole — `rocambole-garlic` · vegetables · rare  
  A different Allium species from cultivated garlic, mild and leek-like rather than pungent, and the part bought is the aerial bulbil rather than a clove.
- **Rosa di Gorizia** / Rosa di Gorizia — `rosa-di-gorizia` · vegetables · luxe  
  A rose-shaped Friulian chicory forced over eight months by a handful of growers, sold at several times the price of Treviso and served raw whole as a plated flower.
- **Roscoff pink onion** / Oignon rosé de Roscoff — `roscoff-pink-onion` · vegetables · both  
  A sweet, low-sulphur AOP onion with pink flesh that holds shape when braised whole, unlike the sharp yellow onion or the raw-salad red onion.
- **Shepherd's purse** / Bourse-à-pasteur — `shepherds-purse` · vegetables · rare  
  A wild brassica green with a nutty, faintly grassy taste, blanched and chopped as a filling rather than served as a leaf.
- **Shishito pepper** / Piment shishito — `shishito` · vegetables · rare  
  Thin-walled and wrinkled where padrón is thick and smooth, it blisters in half the time and stays sweet rather than turning grassy; a different cooking behaviour, not a grade.
- **Sunflower shoots** / Pousses de tournesol — `sunflower-shoots` · vegetables · rare  
  Thick-stemmed seedlings with a hazelnut-and-lemon flavour and enough body to stand up to dressing, unlike a fine microgreen.
- **Tara no me** / Pousse d'aralia — `tara-no-me` · vegetables · luxe  
  The terminal shoot of the angelica tree, the most prized sansai, so bitter it is only ever fried as tempura; a tree bud, not a ground vegetable.
- **Taro** / Taro — `taro` · vegetables  
  A mucilaginous corm that thickens the liquid it cooks in and must be handled with gloves raw; behaves nothing like potato or yam.
- **Taro stem** / Tige de taro — `zuiki` · vegetables · rare  
  The leaf stalk, not the corm — spongy, sold fresh or dried, and bought to soak up dashi; a separate purchase from taro with its own preparation.
- **Tropea red onion** / Oignon rouge de Tropea — `tropea-red-onion` · vegetables · rare  
  An elongated Calabrian IGP onion sweet enough to serve raw or roasted whole as a dish in itself, a use ordinary red onion cannot carry.
- **Urui** / Pousse de hosta — `urui` · vegetables · rare  
  Blanched hosta shoots, crisp and faintly mucilaginous, eaten raw or lightly dressed; the only sansai in this set with no bitterness.
- **Winter melon** / Courge cireuse — `winter-melon` · vegetables · rare  
  Cooks to a translucent, near-flavourless gel that carries broth; bought for clarity and texture in banquet soups, not for taste.
- **Yardlong bean** / Haricot kilomètre — `yardlong-bean` · vegetables  
  A cowpea, not a common bean: denser and chewier, it wants dry-frying or long braising and turns limp under the quick blanch that suits a green bean.
