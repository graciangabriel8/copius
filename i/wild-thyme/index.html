Wild thyme

Serpolet

Latin nameThymus serpyllum
FamilyHerbs
OriginLimestone and alpine pasture
SeasonJune – August
Flavourherbal · woody · floral
Typical price1,50–4 €/botte

What it is

Serpyllum comes from the Latin serpere, to creep, and the plant does exactly that, flattening itself across limestone and alpine turf instead of making a bush. It carries far less thymol than garden thyme, which is why a whole flowering stem can go into a dish where common thyme would have to be stripped and rationed.

In the kitchen

Use the flowering stem whole and lift it out at the end, as you would a bay leaf. It gives itself up to warm honey or oil in twenty minutes off the heat; boiling drives off the floral top notes first.
