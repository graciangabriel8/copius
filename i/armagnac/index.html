Armagnac

Latin nameVitis vinifera 'Baco'
FamilyCellar
OriginGascony, southwest France
SeasonAll year
Flavourfruity · woody · warm · rich
Typical price40–100 €/L

What it is

Baco 22A is a hybrid, crossed at the end of the nineteenth century in the wake of phylloxera, and Armagnac is the one French appellation still allowed to keep a hybrid in the vineyard. A single low-strength run through the alambic armagnacais leaves in the heavy aromatics that a second distillation would strip, which is why it reads as prune and violet where Cognac reads as flowers.

In the kitchen

Those extra congeners survive heat, so it holds where Cognac vanishes: a spoonful worked into the forcemeat of a duck or pork terrine before it goes into the mould, or poured over prunes left to swell overnight. Do not flambe the old bottles - the wood is what you paid for, and the flame takes it first.
