Huauzontle

Latin nameChenopodium berlandieri
FamilyVegetables
OriginValley of Mexico
SeasonFebruary – May
Flavourearthy · grassy · bitter
Typical price25–45 €/kg

What it is

The Nahuatl name joins huauhtli, amaranth, to tzontli, hair — the plant is a goosefoot grown for its whole flowering head rather than its leaves or its grain. Bound into bundles around a stick of cheese, battered and fried, it is a Lenten dish in central Mexico, eaten by stripping the buds off the stalk with your teeth.

In the kitchen

Whisk the whites to a foam, fold the yolks back in, and fry the bundle seam-side down first so it seals — a flat beaten egg slides off. Warn the table that the central stalk stays in and is not eaten.
